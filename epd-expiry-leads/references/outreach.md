# Outreach

Read this before sending anything to anyone in the list.

## Timing

California requires the EPD to be **valid at installation**, not at bid
or at purchase. So an EPD that expires in nine months can already fail a
bid being made today, by a project installing in eleven months.

First contact therefore belongs 9 to 12 months before expiry. The
`contact_by` column in the workbook is that date, computed as twelve
months before the earliest expiry for the company. Contact at or after
`contact_by` and you are late; a manufacturer who has already renewed
does not need a platform.

Three emails: day 0, day 5, day 14. Then stop. The window closes when
the EPD expires.

## CAN-SPAM

Business email to a business address is covered by CAN-SPAM, which means
even a cold unsolicited email to a named employee has to comply:

- Accurate headers. No false `From`, no mismatched `Reply-To`.
- A valid physical postal address for the sender.
- A working opt-out, honoured within 10 business days.
- No harvested addresses used for anything other than the message they
  were collected for.

The postal address requirement is the one people forget. A B2B lead list
of names and emails is not enough on its own; the sending setup needs the
address.

## CASL for Canadian contacts

Canada's law is stricter and consent-based, and it applies to the
Canadian contacts in this list. A business-to-business email needs
consent or a legitimate-interest basis, and the commercial electronic
message needs identification of the sender, a physical address and an
unsubscribe mechanism.

The `us_based` column separates US registrants from the rest. Ecobat's
registrant is in Egypt, Teknion's is in Canada, Erie Architectural
Products is Canadian. Do not treat them as US prospects.

## Non-affiliation

Say plainly that ClimatePoint is not affiliated with the programme
operator. Do not use a programme operator's logo, and do not imply the
message comes from the programme.

The point is not only legal. A manufacturer who thinks the programme
operator is chasing them will not read the message, and a manufacturer
who finds out later will remember it.

Suggested wording:

> This message is from ClimatePoint, an independent LCA software company.
> We are not affiliated with NSF or with any Buy Clean programme, and we
> are not contacting you on their behalf.

## What to say

The expiry is the reason to talk, and the reason to talk early is that
the deadline is real.

A working opening:

> Your [product] EPD is valid until [date]. Because California requires
> the EPD to be valid at installation rather than at bid, an EPD that
> lapses in [month] stops you bidding work that installs in [month] and
> later. We help manufacturers renew or replace EPDs in [time to renewal]
> rather than [time to renewal] at the last minute.

Then the pitch, which depends on what the lead list says about them:

- **In-house preparer (+2)** - they have an LCA team. Pitch the workflow
  and the data model, not the concept of EPDs.
- **Consultant-authored (-2)** - a consultancy will probably drive the
  renewal. The pitch is about working alongside the consultancy, or
  about cost and turnaround, and about who owns the underlying model.
- **Supplier-authored (-2)** - the author sells them inputs. The EPD
  supports the supplier. Worth a careful first email, because the
  manufacturer may not have a strong view of the EPD at all.
- **Five or more EPDs expiring (+2)** - they have a programme to run.
  Pitch a batch.
- **Buy Clean state (+2)** - name the state. The other programme is a
  deadline too.

## Before you send

- Check for a newer EPD from the same company. A listing can point at a
  document the manufacturer has already replaced, and a lead who renewed
  last month is not a lead.
- Prefer a named contact. The PDF often names a sustainability or
  product stewardship person directly, and a named person is worth more
  than a generic address.
- Verify the address is a working role address for that person. A
  `sustainability@` alias is fine for a role-based approach, less fine
  for a claim that a specific individual owns the EPD.
