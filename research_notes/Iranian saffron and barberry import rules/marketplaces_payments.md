# Marketplaces, Payment Providers and Payment Channels for Iranian-Origin Saffron and Barberries (EU/US), as of October 2026

Scope note: the research date is 18 October 2026. Items dated before September 2025 (the EU/UN "snapback") are labelled HISTORICAL. Amazon Seller Central, Etsy and PayPal help pages could not be fetched directly: they render with JavaScript, or returned 403 or truncated content. Where Amazon policy text is quoted, it comes from an Amazon staff reply on the Seller Central forum or from secondary sources. Verify the live Seller Central text in a logged-in account before relying on it.

## 1. Amazon (US and EU marketplaces): sanctions/origin policy, seller identity, grocery gating

### Takeaway
Amazon's "Trade Control Laws" policy bans the sale of items "imported from or originating in" Iran. This is a product-origin ban, not only a ban on shipping to Iran. Amazon enforces it by asking sellers for a certificate of origin. No source found shows that the EU storefronts are exempt. Iranian-origin saffron or zereshk therefore cannot lawfully be listed on Amazon.com or on Amazon.de/.fr/.es/.it/.nl, whether shipped directly from Iran or re-routed through Turkey. Misdeclaring the origin would breach the policy and risk account suspension.

### Cited Findings
- An Amazon staff member (Nikki_Amazon) quoted the policy verbatim on the Seller Central forum, about two years before October 2026: "Amazon's Trade Control Laws policy states that items imported from or originating in Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and the Crimea region of Ukraine are not allowed to be sold on Amazon." The same staffer told a seller whose rug-pattern pillow covers had been flagged: "At this time, a certificate of origin or certificate of compliance are the requested documents to get this resolved." So Amazon flags suspected sanctioned-origin listings and asks for origin documents. — [Amazon Seller Forums thread "Rug Design on a set of Pillow Covers Incorrectly Flagged for Restricted Products Sanctions Violations"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/06d05054-88f4-4ffa-84a2-87a8fef991a1)
- A guest post by Amazon-seller lawyer Robert Segall (TaxJar, dated 1 December 2024; originally c. 2020) quotes "Amazon's general policy that items imported from or originating in the following countries and regions" and lists Iran, North Korea, Sudan and the Crimea Region of Ukraine. It also states that "Sellers who violate these laws can be and are suspended from Amazon all the time" and that "Even if it is unknowingly, Amazon Sellers who violate sanction laws are at risk for Amazon suspensions." — [TaxJar: What Amazon sellers need to know about Sanctions Law](https://www.taxjar.com/blog/amazon-sanctions-law)
- Country lists differ between secondary sources: some include Sudan, some Syria, some Donetsk/Luhansk. Every version found includes Iran. — [TaxJar](https://www.taxjar.com/blog/amazon-sanctions-law); [Bright Vessel](https://www.brightvessel.com/amazon-restricts-products-according-to-the-country/); [Amazon forum](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/06d05054-88f4-4ffa-84a2-87a8fef991a1)
- A 2026 seller-registration guide claims Amazon "doesn't just check your registration country - it audits sourcing." It also claims a sanctioned factory address on a commercial invoice leads to suspension. This is a third-party assertion, not Amazon text. — [Feedvisor: Countries Approved by Amazon, 2026](https://feedvisor.com/university/countries-approved-by-amazon/)
- A German-language FAQ, not an official Amazon page, paraphrases the policy for Amazon.de: "Artikel, die aus den folgenden Regionen und Ländern stammen oder dorthin gehen, dürfen nicht auf Amazon verkauft werden" (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Sudan, Crimea). No official Seller Central Europe page could be retrieved. — [kuechenfibel.de FAQ](https://www.kuechenfibel.de/faq/in-welchem-land-ist-amazon-nicht-verfugbar)
- HISTORICAL (2020): OFAC fined Amazon.com Inc. US$134,523 for apparent violations of several sanctions programmes. The violations included goods and services provided to persons in Crimea, Iran and Syria between 2011 and 2018, and orders for embassy staff of sanctioned countries. OFAC attributed them to screening "deficiencies"; one example was a "Yalta, Krimea" address that was not flagged. — [OFAC settlement notice](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/44311/download?inline=); [Shutts & Bowen summary](https://www.shutts.com/business-and-legal-insights/compliance-with-u-s-export-sanctions-is)
- HISTORICAL (2017): Amazon disclosed in an SEC filing that it had sold consumer goods to Iranian-government-linked buyers. It said it had "voluntarily reported these orders" to OFAC and BIS. — [CNN Money, 1 Aug 2017](https://money.cnn.com/2017/08/01/news/companies/amazon-iran-sanctions-investigation/index.html); [phys.org/AP](https://phys.org/news/2017-08-amazon-federal-iran-linked-sales.html)
- HISTORICAL (2019): In August 2019 Amazon Web Services disabled accounts of Iran-based users. An AWS spokesperson said Amazon does not serve customers in Iran because Iran "is subject to broad trade restrictions, limiting virtually all business with Iran." Coverage concerned AWS and Iran-resident users, not marketplace sellers in the EU. — [Iran International: Amazon cuts services to Iran](https://old.iranintl.com/en/world/amazon-cuts-services-iran); [Al Jazeera, 2 Oct 2019: "Locked out: why is Amazon blocking Iranians from its services"](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2019/10/2/locked-out-why-is-amazon-blocking-iranians-from-its-services); [Center for Human Rights in Iran, Aug 2019](https://iranhumanrights.org/2019/08/more-iranians-forced-to-rely-on-unsafe-online-hosting-after-amazon/)
- US law reaches US-owned foreign subsidiaries. Under 31 CFR 560.215, "an entity that is owned or controlled by a United States person" and established outside the US is prohibited from knowingly engaging in Iran transactions that would be prohibited for a US person. — [31 CFR 560.215 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.215)
- Grocery gating on Amazon US: Amazon's own seller blog says grocery sellers need category approval and lists "compliance documentation from government entities like the FDA or USDA" and a business license among application items. Third-party guides say approval is "per seller, per category, per marketplace", needs a Professional account, and has performance thresholds: ODR <1%, pre-fulfilment cancel rate <2.5%, late shipment <4%. Brand owners may need FDA facility registration, HACCP/GFSI certification, GTINs, and FSVP/Prior Notice for imported food. — [Amazon: How to sell groceries in the Amazon store](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/how-to-sell-groceries-amazon); [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/how-to-sell-groceries-on-amazon/); [ecombrainly (coffee guide)](https://ecombrainly.com/how-to-sell-coffee-on-amazon/)
- Grocery gating on Amazon.de: German seller guides disagree. One lists "Lebensmittel und Gourmet-Lebensmittel" as an approval-required category; another says only some subcategories are gated. Food-safety certificates and business licences are reportedly requested. — [repricer.com (DE)](https://www.repricer.com/de/blog/amazon-gated-categories/); [Helium 10 (DE)](https://www.helium10.com/language/german/was-kann-ich-ohne-genehmigung-auf-amazon-verkaufen/); [AliDrop (DE)](https://www.alidrop.co/de/blogs/tiefkuhlkost-auf-amazon)

### Inferences
- Amazon.com Inc. owns Amazon's EU marketplace operator, which is therefore within 31 CFR 560.215. Amazon applies one global "Trade Control Laws" policy. So Iranian-origin goods are almost certainly barred on EU storefronts too, even though EU law itself does not ban importing Iranian saffron (see section 3).
- Routing goods through Turkey does not change origin for Amazon's purposes. Drying, sorting and repacking saffron or barberries is unlikely to be "substantial transformation." Amazon asks for certificates of origin, and a Turkish certificate of origin for Iranian-grown saffron would be false. Labelling the product "Turkish" or "Spanish" to get past Amazon would breach the policy and risk permanent suspension with funds withheld. It could also breach EU food-labelling and customs rules and amount to fraud.
- "Persian saffron", "Iranian", "Birjand" or "zereshk" in listing text is likely to trigger automated sanctions flags. Forum reports show flags on items with Persian or Iranian design references.
- No source found says Amazon bans sellers solely for Iranian nationality when they reside in the EU. Seller verification (passport/ID, residence, bank account) runs against sanctions screening, though, and Iran is not an eligible seller-registration country. Read this as heightened scrutiny, not a confirmed nationality ban.

### Gaps
- Exact current wording of Amazon's "Export Controls"/"Trade Control Laws" help pages on sellercentral.amazon.com and sellercentral-europe.amazon.com. The pages are JavaScript-only and could not be fetched.
- No verified reports found of Amazon closing EU marketplace seller accounts in 2019 (or later) solely because the seller was Iranian or Iran-linked. The 2019 coverage found concerns AWS and Iran-resident users.
- No source retrieved for the 2026 Amazon EU grocery approval document list, for example proof of food business registration under Reg (EC) 852/2004 or lab tests.
- Amazon's country-of-origin attribute rules for EU food listings were not found in primary text.

## 2. Other channels: Etsy, eBay, Stripe/Shopify, PayPal, EU marketplaces and EU payment providers

### Takeaway
The large US-headquartered platforms and processors (Etsy, eBay, Stripe, and therefore Shopify Payments where Stripe runs it) explicitly ban goods originating in Iran, regardless of where the seller is. eBay also bars accounts for entities based in Iran or on the SDN list. No explicit Iran-origin product ban was found for EU-headquartered providers (Adyen, Mollie, Klarna, Bol.com). Adyen's public list only references "applicable laws", and Mollie, Klarna and Bol.com policies were not found. An own webshop on an EU-based payment service provider is the only channel not shown to be closed by policy text, but bank and card-scheme de-risking may still close it.

### Cited Findings
- **Etsy.** Its help page, as captured in search snippets because the page returned 403, says: "Etsy does not allow the sale of items that originate from sanctioned countries and regions," and the ban applies "regardless of the date they were removed from the sanctioned country or region." Sellers may be asked to disclose an item's country of origin. Narrow exemptions cover informational materials (publications, films, posters, photographs, records/CDs) and certain artworks. Etsy says it "cannot authorize items or transactions as being specially exempt" as a third-party venue. — [Etsy Help: How can I follow Etsy's Sanction Policy?](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/23786242550039-How-can-I-Follow-Etsy-s-Sanction-Policy)
- HISTORICAL (Jan 2020): the National Iranian American Council complained that Etsy removed US-made "Persian" items and treated Iranian-Americans in a discriminatory way. This shows aggressive keyword enforcement. — [NIAC letter to Etsy](https://niacouncil.org/niac-letter-regarding-etsys-u-s-sanctions-compliance-policies-discriminatory-treatment-towards-iranian-americans/)
- **eBay** (US policy page, © 1995-2026). "Goods from embargoed countries aren't allowed." "Sellers can't list embargoed goods or pre-embargoed goods from these countries or regions:" Crimea, Cuba, North Korea, DNR, Iran, Kherson, LNR, Syria, Zaporizhzhia. Organisations, businesses or individuals based in those places, or on the US SDN list, "can't register accounts on eBay." The Australian version is the same and was updated 23 Sep 2025. — [eBay Embargoed goods and prohibited countries policy](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/embargoed-goods-prohibited-countries-policy?id=4323); [eBay.com.au version](https://www.ebay.com.au/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/embargoed-goods-policy?id=4323); [eBay.de version](https://wwww.ebay.de/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/embargoed-goods-policy?id=4323)
- eBay forum anecdote (2019): listings were removed under the "Embargoed goods policy" apparently just for the word "Persian". — [eBay Community thread](https://community.ebay.com/t5/Selling/Policy-Enforcement-embargoed-countries-needs-to-learn-to-read-a/m-p/33672033)
- **Stripe.** Its Restricted Businesses list (© 2026) prohibits "Use of Stripe's services for any dealings, engagement, or sale of goods or services" linked to "products or services originating from jurisdictions that Stripe has determined for various reasons", "including, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, and Syria, and the Crimea, Donetsk, and Luhansk regions." It also bars dealings with persons on the restricted lists of the "United States, United Kingdom, European Union", "including the sanctions maintained by the US Office of Foreign Assets Control." — [Stripe Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/en-gb-ca/legal/restricted-businesses); [Stripe support: Restricted businesses in Mexico](https://support.stripe.com/questions/restricted-businesses-in-mexico)
- **Shopify Payments.** Shopify does not state its own country rules here. Its processor list sends merchants to each underlying processor's prohibited/restricted business policy, which includes Stripe's and PayPal's. — [Shopify: Payment Processors for Shopify Payments](https://www.shopify.com/legal/processor-list)
- **Adyen** (Dutch). Its public "Prohibited and Restricted products & services" page has no country-specific sanctions list. It cites "Local, national and international laws" and requires compliance with "Applicable Laws, regulations and scheme rules." A third-party fintech (Relay) says Adyen's "global risk policies" block card payments where an owner is a citizen or resident of listed countries; Iran's presence on that list was not visible. — [Adyen restricted/prohibited list](https://adyen.com/zh_CN/legal/list-restricted-prohibited); [Relay help article](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/38752882026772-Countries-Not-Eligible-for-Card-Payments-with-Invoices-or-Quick-Requests/)
- **PayPal.** The Acceptable Use Policy page could not be retrieved (truncated). Only low-quality third-party blogs say Iran is unsupported "under international sanctions". Treat as unverified. — [cs-cart blog](https://www.cs-cart.com/blog/paypal-c)
- **Tradera** (Swedish marketplace). Its "goods from sanctioned countries" page lists Iran among restricted origins. This shows that an EU-based marketplace can also ban Iranian-origin goods. — [Tradera support](https://www.tradera.com/support/gb/posts/varor-fran-sanktionerade-laender/)

### Inferences
- Etsy, eBay, Stripe (and Shopify Payments, which runs on Stripe in most EU countries and the US) and very likely PayPal are closed to Iranian-origin saffron and barberries by explicit policy. This holds even for an EU-resident seller shipping to EU buyers, because these companies apply OFAC-based global policies. PayPal's and Stripe's EU entities are US-owned (31 CFR 560.215).
- An own webshop with an EU-headquartered payment service provider (Mollie, Adyen, Klarna) is the channel least clearly barred by written policy. Two risks remain. First, card schemes and acquirers screen merchant descriptions. Second, the merchant's EU bank may refuse to hold proceeds from Iran-origin trade or to pay the Iranian supplier. Disclose the product origin to the provider during onboarding instead of concealing it.
- Selling to US customers from any channel is a separate problem. Importing Iranian-origin food into the US is prohibited (section 4), so EU webshops should geo-block US delivery.

### Gaps
- No policy text found for Bol.com, Zalando-type EU marketplaces, Kaufland.de, Cdiscount, Allegro, Mollie or Klarna on Iranian-origin goods or Iran-connected merchants. Read their merchant terms directly.
- No current PayPal Acceptable Use Policy text retrieved.
- No evidence found either way on whether Etsy, eBay or Stripe bar an Iranian national resident in the EU (as distinct from Iran-resident persons). eBay's text bars only those "based in" Iran or on the SDN list.

## 3. Paying the farmer in Iran from the EU: legal rules (post-snapback), de-risking, and channels

### Takeaway
EU law (Reg. 267/2012 as re-amended on 29 Sept 2025 by Reg. 2025/1975) does not ban buying Iranian saffron or barberries. Food payments to Iran remain lawful in principle. Below €10,000 there is no notification requirement. From €10,000, food transfers need prior notification, and transfers involving Iranian banks need prior authorisation from €100,000. In Germany the notification goes to the Bundesbank, filed by the payment service provider. In practice the route is largely closed: most major Iranian banks and EIH Hamburg are asset-frozen, EU banks may not open new correspondent relationships with Iranian banks, and EU banks broadly refuse Iran payments. The Turkish route has also tightened sharply after the US "Operation Economic Outcast" (Aug-Sept 2026). Hawala and unlicensed money transfer are criminal offences for the operator in Germany.

### Cited Findings
**Legal framework after the snapback**
- On 29 Sept 2025 the EU Council reintroduced its Iran nuclear sanctions by amending Reg. 267/2012. Reg. (EU) 2025/1975 covers trade, finance and transport; Implementing Regs 2025/1980 and 2025/1982 cover asset freezes. The measures have no retroactive effect. — [Mayer Brown, Oct 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/snap-decision-renewed-global-iran-sanctions-and-increased-risks-for-business/); [financialcrime.lu, 29 Sep 2025](https://financialcrime.lu/2025/09/29/EU-Iran-Sanctions-Snapback/)
- Funds-transfer thresholds per Mayer Brown's summary of Arts 30/30a. Transfers involving "Iran-Related Institutions" (Iranian banks/bureaux de change, their branches, and foreign institutions controlled by Iran-domiciled persons):
  - Food, healthcare, medical equipment, agricultural or humanitarian purposes: no restriction below €10,000; advance notification from €10,000 to under €100,000; prior authorisation from €100,000.
  - Personal remittances: notification from €10,000 to under €40,000; authorisation from €40,000.
  - Other purposes: authorisation from €10,000.
  - Transfers to Iranian persons not via Iran-Related Institutions: food/agricultural transfers need only advance notification above €10,000, with no authorisation tier; other purposes need notification from €10,000 to under €40,000 and authorisation from €40,000.
  - Thresholds apply to single operations or several operations that "appear to be linked."
  — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback); confirmed in outline by [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/corporate-governance-compliance/art/eu-iran-reimposing-sanctions) and [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions)
- No import ban on Iranian foodstuffs. EU import bans cover crude oil and petroleum products (Annex IV), natural gas (IVa), petrochemicals (V), gold, precious metals and diamonds from the Government of Iran (VII), and dual-use/proliferation items. Neither Mayer Brown nor Covington lists any food or agricultural import ban. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions)
- Banks re-listed by the EU include the Central Bank of Iran, Bank Melli, Bank Mellat, Bank Saderat, Sina Bank, and "the Europäisch-Iranische Handelsbank (EIH) in Germany" (a non-exhaustive list). Covington: about 50 natural persons and nearly 200 entities are reinstated in Annexes VIII/IX, "notably" major financial institutions. EU financial institutions are prohibited from "opening new accounts for Iranian banks" and "entering into correspondent banking relationships with Iranian banks." — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback)
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/776 of 30 March 2026 updated the EU Iran asset-freeze list. This comes from a law-firm blog summary; the primary text was not checked. — [cambioslegales.es, 2026](https://www.cambioslegales.es/en/blog/eu-sanctions-iran-2026-exporters-financial-institutions-1)
- **Germany: Bundesbank procedure.** Transfers "von und an iranische Personen, Organisationen oder Einrichtungen ab EUR 10.000,00 bis unter EUR 40.000,00" must be notified to the Bundesbank as competent authority, and "bedürfen ab EUR 40.000,00 der Genehmigung." For electronic transfers, notifications and applications are filed by "dem Zahlungsverkehrsdienstleister des Auftraggebers bzw. Begünstigten der Zahlung." The page notes exemptions for transfers concerning "Lebensmitteln, Gesundheitsleistungen, medizinischer Ausrüstung" and "landwirtschaftlichen oder humanitären Zwecken." — [Deutsche Bundesbank: Finanzsanktionen Iran](https://www.bundesbank.de/de/service/finanzsanktionen/sanktionsregimes/-/iran-610216)
- **EIH Bank Hamburg.** US-designated since 2010. Re-listed by the EU after the snapback (Covington) and listed by the UK in an October 2025 notice (Depenau 2, Hamburg). It is not a usable channel. — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions); [UK OFSI notice, Oct 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68df91868c1db6022d0ca092/Notice_Iran__Nuclear__031025.pdf); [moneylaundering.com on the 2010 US designation](https://www.moneylaundering.com/news/u-s-treasury-department-designates-hamburg-bank-with-iran-ties)
- **INSTEX** (HISTORICAL). The E3 set it up in 2019 for humanitarian trade. It processed only one transaction (medical goods, early 2020). Its 10 shareholder states decided to liquidate it on 9 March 2023. — [French MFA, 9 Mar 2023](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/country-files/iran/news/article/the-10-instex-shareholder-states-have-decided-to-liquidate-instex-due-to); [UK gov press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/the-10-instex-shareholder-states-have-decided-to-liquidate-instex-due-to-continued-obstruction-from-iran)

**De-risking in practice (2026)**
- A Jerusalem Post analysis (opinion, ~Oct 2026) says EU/OFAC exemptions for food and medicine are "largely inert" because "tier-one Western banks routinely block them." — [JPost: "The financial siege"](http://www.jpost.com/jerusalem-report/article-911501)
- HISTORICAL (c. 2020): ECFR reported that European banks refused even routine SEPA transfers with an Iran nexus, citing shareholder and creditor fears. — [ECFR: Bankless task](https://ecfr.eu/article/commentary_bankless_task_can_europe_stay_connected_to_iran/)
- **US "Operation Economic Outcast"** (24 Aug 2026). Treasury Secretary Bessent announced expanded secondary sanctions: close to 60 designations, suspended licences, and extension into shipping, aviation, gold, technology and digital assets. "Any entity laundering money on Iran's behalf will be removed from the dollar system." Euronews: "Washington is effectively asking every firm on the planet to choose between Iran and the American financial system." The EU Blocking Statute still bars EU firms from complying with US Iran sanctions without Commission authorisation. EU-Iran goods trade was €3.72bn in 2025, and EU imports from Iran are "dominated by pistachios and other food products." — [Euronews, 25 Aug 2026](https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/08/25/what-the-new-us-sanctions-on-iran-mean-for-european-businesses)

**Turkey route**
- On 4 Sept 2026 OFAC designated Golden Global Yatırım Bankası (Turkey) and two subsidiaries for facilitating "tens of millions of dollars" for the IRGC-Quds Force and for moving Iranian oil revenue from China to Turkey to convert into cash and gold. This was the second bank targeted under Operation Economic Outcast. The day before, Halkbank announced a settlement with the US DOJ in its long-running Iran sanctions case. — [CNBC, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/iran-turkey-sanctions-economy-bessent.html); [Al Jazeera, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/5/us-imposes-sanctions-on-turkish-bank-prompting-legal-threat); [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/amp/iran-turkey-bank-sanctions-treasury-economic-outcast/33847194.html); [Turkish Minute](https://www.turkishminute.com/2026/09/04/us-treasury-sanctions-turkish-bank-over-alleged-iran-ties/)
- Unverified single source (opinion piece): Turkish regulators revoked the licence of Bank Mellat's Istanbul branch on 18 Sept 2026. — [Ynet opinion](https://www.ynetnews.com/opinions-analysis/article/s1ajzltyze)

**Exchange houses**
- By Sept 2026 the US had sanctioned 20 Iranian exchange companies, six of them bank-owned. Iran International (Oct 2026) reports that Parsian Exchange is 90% owned by Bank Parsian (US-designated Oct 2018) and is therefore blocked for US persons under the 50% rule. It moved funds through UAE, China and Hong Kong layers involving Saman, Shahr, Eghtesad Novin, Tourism Bank, Melli and Mellat. — [Iran International, Oct 2026](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610071494); [US Treasury press release sb0596](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0596)

**Hawala / informal transfer (Germany)**
- Offering payment services without a ZAG licence is a criminal offence: up to 5 years' imprisonment for intent, up to 3 years for negligence. The illicit or licit origin of the money does not matter. Hawala business models "cannot be granted a licence" in Germany or the EU because they lack full customer identification. The BGH confirmed convictions in 2025 (3 StR 111/24). — [Ferner Alsdorf: BGH zur Strafbarkeit von Hawala (2025)](https://www.ferner-alsdorf.de/bundesgerichtshof-erneut-zur-strafbarkeit-von-hawala-systemen-2025/)
- According to the federal government's answers to parliamentary questions, the hawala operator commits the offence; using hawala is not punishable for the sender or recipient. A June/July 2026 government answer repeats that the offence is the unlicensed financial transfer business itself. — [Bundestag Drucksache 20/12058](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/20/120/2012058.pdf); [hib 540/2026, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1193200); [Hessenschau: Darmstadt hawala conviction](https://www.hessenschau.de/panorama/illegaler-geld-transfer-zwei-maenner-im-hawala-prozess-in-darmstadt-verurteilt-v1,hawala-prozess-100.html)

**Iran's own currency rules**
- HISTORICAL (2018-2020): a Central Bank of Iran circular obliges "all exporters of goods and services" to repatriate export foreign exchange into the Iranian economy, through the NIMA system or the exchange centre. Penalties for defaulters included the Industry Ministry refusing to issue or renew their commercial cards (kart-e bazargani), customs and tax restrictions, naming and shaming, and judicial referral. — [Nour Law](https://nourlaw.com/foreign-currency-repatriation-obligation-set-for-iranian-exporters/); [Radio Farda, 2020](https://en.radiofarda.com/a/iran-to-force-exporters-to-repatriate-their-hard-currency-earnings-amid-devaluation/30709436.html); [Eghtesad Online, Jul 2020](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/750428/irans-cb-puts-exporters-on-notice)
- In 2026 the CBI is raising the official rate (from about 137,000 to over 174,000 tomans in 2026) to coax exporters to repatriate. A $2bn reserve injection is under debate. Non-oil exports to mid-August 2026 were about $15bn, nearly 30% below the prior year. — [Iran International, Oct 2026](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610076990); [Iran International, Sep 2026](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609018792)
- Per a Tehran Times report (26 Sep 2026), seen via search summary only because the page returned 503: exporters may now sell FX to the CBI for resale to importers and may return currency as banknotes, and more than $1.5bn had been returned under the reforms. JPost describes emergency circulars pushing exporters to repatriate at government rates via NIMA. — [Tehran Times](https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/530429/Central-Bank-takes-6-key-measures-to-facilitate-foreign-trade); [JPost](http://www.jpost.com/jerusalem-report/article-911501)

### Inferences
- **Lawful-in-principle EU route.** An EU-resident buyer (ideally an EU company registered as a food business operator) pays the Iranian seller from an EU bank account, with documented food purpose. Payments ≥ €10,000, or linked payments, must be notified via the bank to the competent authority (in Germany, the Bundesbank). The recipient bank must not be on Annex VIII/IX, which excludes Melli, Mellat, Saderat, Sina and the CBI, and no listed person may benefit. The practical blocker is that few or no EU banks will execute the transfer: correspondent relationships with Iranian banks are prohibited and de-risking is widespread.
- **The farmer's export formalities.** If the farmer sells for export, Iran requires an exporter with a commercial card, customs export declaration and FX repatriation through Iran's system. A small farmer usually sells through a licensed exporter or trading company holding a commercial card. Iranian rules on informal "suitcase" exports or small parcels were not verified.
- **Turkish intermediary.** A Turkish company buys from Iran (Turkey→Iran payment) and invoices the EU buyer, who pays the Turkish company. This removes the direct EU-Iran transfer but not the Iranian origin, which must still be declared on EU customs entry and on the label. If the structure is used to avoid the Art 30/30a notification duty or to hide Iranian beneficiaries from the EU bank, it risks breaching EU anti-circumvention rules and bank terms. It also faces sharply higher Turkish bank de-risking after Sept 2026. Lawful only if fully transparent; high risk.
- **Exchange houses.** Iranian exchange houses (sarrafi) increasingly carry US designations or are 50%+ owned by designated banks. EU-licensed money transmitters generally do not serve Iran. Using an Iranian exchange house via the UAE or Turkey exposes the buyer to dealings with designated persons, which is an EU offence if the entity is EU-listed and a US secondary-sanctions risk otherwise.
- **Hawala/cash.** Operating a hawala is illegal in Germany and the EU. German authorities say the user is not criminally liable, but users still face reporting and confiscation risks, bank account closure, and possible liability under sanctions law if funds reach listed persons. EU cash-declaration rules at borders (≥ €10,000) also apply. Classify as illegal or high-risk; not recommended.
- **Small amounts.** Single, genuinely unlinked food-purpose payments below €10,000 need no notification under EU law. Splitting a larger purchase into sub-€10,000 tranches is caught by the "appear to be linked" aggregation rule and would be a breach.

### Gaps
- Consolidated EUR-Lex text of Arts 30/30a after Reg. 2025/1975 was not fetched; the thresholds come from Mayer Brown, Covington and Cuatrecasas summaries.
- No list was found of which Iranian banks are not EU-listed after the snapback and could legally receive EU transfers.
- No named EU bank or payment institution was found that currently (2026) processes small food payments to Iran.
- Current (2026) Iranian rules not found: FX repatriation deadlines, the minimum export value requiring a commercial card, and treatment of small consignments.
- No 2025-2026 source checked on whether Turkish banks (e.g. Ziraat, Vakıf) still clear Iran trade payments for food.

## 4. Payments and sales involving the US / US persons

### Takeaway
US persons, wherever they live, cannot buy, import, sell, finance or facilitate Iranian-origin saffron or barberries, including via Turkey. US-owned foreign subsidiaries (Amazon EU, Stripe/PayPal EU entities, eBay, Etsy) are bound too. If either partner is a US citizen or green-card holder, that partner cannot take part even in an EU-only business. Selling the goods to US customers is an unlawful import into the US.

### Cited Findings
- 31 CFR 560.215 extends the ITSR prohibitions to "an entity that is owned or controlled by a United States person" established outside the US. — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.215)
- Under 31 CFR 560.407, Iranian-origin raw materials may be imported from third countries only if a non-US person "substantially transformed" them there. Transactions in Iranian-origin goods "not incorporated or transformed in a third country are prohibited." — [Cornell LII 560.407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.407)
- HISTORICAL: from January 2016 a JCPOA general licence (31 CFR 560.534) authorised importing, and dealing in, certain Iranian-origin foodstuffs and carpets from Iran or third countries. The retrieved text names pistachios and caviar as examples; saffron was not confirmed in it. — [OFAC document](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/20281/download)
- HISTORICAL (2010): under CISADA, OFAC "cannot authorize" the commercial import of Iranian-origin goods including foodstuffs and carpets after 29 Sept 2010 (pre-JCPOA position). — [OFAC guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/17641/download)
- OFAC considers goods transshipped through Iran to be Iranian-origin. — [OFAC letter](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/29491/download)
- Aug-Sept 2026: US secondary sanctions expanded ("Operation Economic Outcast"), licences suspended, and non-US banks (e.g. Turkey's Golden Global) designated for Iran facilitation. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/08/25/what-the-new-us-sanctions-on-iran-mean-for-european-businesses); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/iran-turkey-sanctions-economy-bessent.html)
- HISTORICAL (2020): OFAC's Amazon settlement shows that US platforms face enforcement even for small-value Iran-linked orders. — [OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/44311/download?inline=)

### Inferences
- Iranian-origin food cannot be lawfully paid for in USD or through US banks or processors. Any USD-cleared payment touches the US financial system and would be blocked. EUR payments through EU banks with US exposure face the same internal block.
- The Iranian-national partner: if they are a US lawful permanent resident, they are a US person and bound by the ITSR even while living in the EU. If they are EU-resident with Iranian (and possibly EU) nationality, US primary sanctions do not bind them personally. US platforms will still refuse the goods.

### Gaps
- Revocation of the 560.534 foodstuff general licence was not directly verified in this session. My background knowledge is that it was revoked in June 2018 with a wind-down ending 6 Aug 2018; confirm in the Federal Register.
- The ITSR general licence for noncommercial personal (family) remittances to Iran (believed to be 31 CFR 560.550) was not verified in this session. Such remittances would not cover commercial payment for goods anyway.

## 5. Documented cases of diaspora businesses selling Iranian saffron online in the EU, and how they handle payments

### Takeaway
No reliable, documented case study was found of a small Iranian-diaspora business lawfully selling Iranian saffron online in the EU, nor any account of its payment set-up. Available material is trade-press interviews and commercial blogs. These consistently name banking as the main obstacle and describe workarounds through third-country intermediaries, exchange houses, couriers and barter.

### Cited Findings

"Many customers of Iran's pistachios and saffron prefer to purchase from markets where it is possible to use LC or other safe money transfer ways." Russia and China were named as major buyers. — [Mundus Agri / Der AUDITOR, 30 Dec 2022](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/saffron-pistachios-sanctions-biggest-problem.n30046.html)
