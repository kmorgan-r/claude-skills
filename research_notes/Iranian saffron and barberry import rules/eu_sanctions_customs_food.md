# EU law on importing and selling Iranian-origin saffron and dried barberries (zereshk), as of October 2026

Scope note: Research date 18 Oct 2026. Sources found reach to about July 2026 for sanctions developments. Where a fact is older, it is labelled HISTORICAL. Where I quote EU legal text that I did not re-fetch in this session (it comes from well-established EUR-Lex text), I mark it "[EUR-Lex text, not re-fetched this session - verify]". The writer should treat those as high-confidence but check them before publication.

## 1. Do EU Iran sanctions (Reg 267/2012 as reinstated after snapback, Reg 359/2011, Reg 2023/1529) ban or restrict imports of Iranian agricultural products, spices or dried fruit? Which goods are banned?

### Takeaway
No EU Iran sanctions instrument bans or licenses the import of Iranian saffron, barberries or other foodstuffs. The import bans reinstated on 30 Sept 2025 cover crude oil and petroleum products, petrochemicals, natural gas, Annex I/II proliferation goods, and gold/precious metals/diamonds from the Iranian government. Food trade is legal in principle. The real constraints are asset freezes (who you deal with) and the payment rules (how you pay).

### Cited Findings
- Snapback timeline: UN sanctions reinstated "effective September 27, at 8 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time" (2025). EU asset-freeze designations took effect 29 Sept 2025; EU sectoral sanctions took effect 30 Sept 2025; transport-service bans apply from 1 Jan 2026 — [Mayer Brown, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback)
- Legal acts: Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1975 of 29 Sept 2025 amends Reg 267/2012 to reinstate sectoral measures; Implementing Regulations 2025/1980 and 2025/1982 reinstate asset-freeze listings — [Council press release 29 Sept 2025](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/09/29/iran-sanctions-snapback-council-reimposes-restrictive-measures/) (page returned 403 to fetch; act numbers confirmed via search summary and law-firm alerts: [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions), [Gorrissen Federspiel](https://gorrissenfederspiel.com/en/eu-reimposes-wide-ranging-sanctions-against-iran/), [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/sanctions-on-iran-reinstated-following-triggering-of-jcpoa-snapback-by-e3/))
- Import bans in Reg 267/2012 as amended by 2025/1975 (from the OJ text) — [Reg (EU) 2025/1975, OJ L 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975):
  - Art 11(1): "It shall be prohibited: (a) to import crude oil or petroleum products into the Union if they: [originate in Iran or have been exported from Iran]... (b) to purchase crude oil or petroleum products which are located in or which originated in Iran;" plus transport and financing bans. Goods listed in Annex IV.
  - Art 13(1): same prohibitions for petrochemical products (Annex V).
  - Art 14a(1): prohibited "to purchase, transport, or import into the Union natural gas which originates in Iran" (Annex IVA).
  - Art 15(1)(b): purchase, import or transport of gold, precious metals and diamonds (Annex VII) prohibited when from the Government of Iran, its public bodies, or entities acting on its behalf.

Not relevant to food.

Note Art 23 (asset freeze) and Arts 30/30a (fund transfers) are in the list — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)

### Inferences
- Saffron (CN 0910 20) and dried barberries (CN 0813) are not in Annexes I, II, IV, IVA, V or VII. Those annexes cover proliferation goods, hydrocarbons/petrochemicals (Ch. 27-29, 39), and gold/precious metals/diamonds (Ch. 71). So importing Iranian saffron/barberries is not prohibited by EU sanctions as of Oct 2026, provided no listed person is involved and payment rules are followed.
- Because there is no import ban, there is no lawful reason to disguise Iranian origin when importing into the EU. Doing so would only add customs-fraud and food-law liability (see Q4, Q5).
- For US sales, the position is opposite (US ITSR bars Iranian-origin goods). That is outside this note; the US researcher should cover it. Routing EU-imported Iranian saffron onward to US customers would not change its Iranian origin.

## 2. Asset freezes, designated persons and due diligence in the supply chain and payment chain

### Takeaway
The legal risk for a small food importer is not the product but the counterparties. No funds may go, directly or indirectly, to anyone listed under Reg 267/2012 (Annexes VIII/IX: Central Bank and major Iranian banks, energy and shipping companies), Reg 359/2011, Reg 2023/1529 Annex III, or the EU terrorist list (now incl. the IRGC). Knowing or intentional circumvention is itself prohibited.

### Cited Findings
- Snapback restored freezes on "more than 50 individuals and close to 200 entities" in Annexes VIII and IX of Reg 267/2012, including "major financial institutions, companies operating in the energy sector and shipping companies" — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback)
- Search summaries describe the reinstated measures as freezing assets of the Central Bank of Iran and major Iranian commercial banks — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/reimposition-of-un-mandated-sanctions-against-iran-and-additional-eu-and-uk-sanctions); [Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/uk-and-eu-reimpose-iranian-sanctions-following-un-snapback/)
- Designated persons are automatically added to existing financial-messaging restrictions; EU institutions face limits on new accounts, correspondent relationships and joint ventures with Iran-related institutions — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback)
- Art 33(1): EU credit and financial institutions may not open new bank accounts with, or establish "a new correspondent banking relationship" with, Iranian institutions; Art 30(6) requires enhanced vigilance and refusal of payment instructions lacking originator/beneficiary information — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- IRGC listed as a terrorist organisation 19 Feb 2026; EU operators barred from making funds available to it — [Council 19 Feb 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/19/eu-terrorist-list-council-designates-the-islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps-as-a-terrorist-organisation/)
- Art 41 bans knowing and intentional participation in activities whose object or effect is to circumvent Art 23 (freeze) and Arts 30/30a (transfers) — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 23(3) of Reg 267/2012: "No funds or economic resources shall be made available, directly or indirectly, to or for the benefit of the natural or legal persons, entities and bodies listed in Annexes VIII and IX." — [EUR-Lex consolidated 267/2012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/267/oj) [EUR-Lex text, not re-fetched this session - verify; the fetch of 2025/1975 did not reproduce Art 23]

### Inferences
- Practical due diligence for this couple: (a) screen the Birjand grower, any Iranian processor/packer, exporter, freight forwarder, shipping line, Turkish intermediary and every bank in the chain against the EU consolidated list (Sanctions Map / Financial Sanctions Database); (b) check ownership/control (EU guidance treats entities owned >50% or controlled by a listed person as covered — standard Council Best Practices position, not re-verified this session); (c) watch for IRGC-linked conglomerates and bonyads that own logistics, packing or trading firms; (d) keep written records.
- EU sanctions apply to EU nationals anywhere, to anyone within EU territory, and to EU-incorporated entities (Art 49 of 267/2012, standard jurisdiction clause; not re-fetched). An Iranian national resident in the EU is bound by EU sanctions like anyone else in the EU. Iranian nationality alone does not make a person "designated".
- Penalties are set nationally. Directive (EU) 2024/1226 harmonises criminal offences for sanctions violations and circumvention (transposition deadline May 2025; not re-verified this session). Member states differ in penalty levels.

## 3. Funds-transfer rules for paying an Iranian supplier (post-snapback), food exemption, competent authorities

### Takeaway
Since 30 Sept 2025, transfers between EU banks and Iranian banks are prohibited unless they fall under listed grounds; foodstuffs and agricultural trade are such a ground. Food-trade payments below EUR 10,000 need nothing; EUR 10,000 to below EUR 100,000 need advance notification; EUR 100,000+ need prior authorisation. Filing is by the payer's (or payee's) payment service provider, to the national competent authority (Germany: Bundesbank). In practice, finding an EU bank willing to process Iran payments is the bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- Art 30(1) (as replaced by 2025/1975): "It shall be prohibited to transfer funds between, on the one hand, financial and credit institutions" in the EU and, on the other, Iranian credit/financial institutions and bureaux de change, unless the transfer falls under Art 30(2) and is processed under Art 30(3) — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30(2)(a) permitted ground: "transfers regarding foodstuffs, healthcare, medical equipment, or for agricultural or humanitarian purposes;" — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30(3)(a): "transfers due on transactions regarding foodstuffs, healthcare, medical equipment, or for agricultural or humanitarian purposes, below EUR 100 000 or equivalent," are carried out without prior authorisation; such transfers of EUR 10,000 or more must be notified in advance; personal remittances below a lower threshold are treated the same way — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30(3)(b): food/humanitarian transfers of EUR 100,000 or more require prior authorisation. Art 30(3)(c): "any other transfer equal to or above EUR 10 000 or equivalent shall require prior authorisation" — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30(4): "Transfers of funds below EUR 10 000 or equivalent shall not require any prior authorisation or notification." — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30(5): "Notifications and requests for authorisations relating to the transfer of funds to an entity ... shall be addressed by or on behalf of the payment service provider of the payer" (to the competent authority of the member state where the transfer is ordered; payee's PSP for incoming) — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Art 30a (transfers to or from an "Iranian person, entity or body" not covered by Art 30(1)): (1)(a) "transfers due on transactions regarding foodstuffs, healthcare, medical equipment, or for agricultural or humanitarian purposes shall be carried out without any prior authorisation" (notification at EUR 10,000+); (1)(c) "any other transfer equal to or above EUR 40 000 or equivalent shall require a prior authorisation" — [Reg 2025/1975](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202501975)
- Mayer Brown summary: for Iran-related institutions, food/humanitarian transfers need nothing below EUR 10,000, advance notification from EUR 10,000 to below EUR 100,000, prior authorisation at EUR 100,000+; personal remittances use EUR 10,000/EUR 40,000 cutoffs; other transfers to/from Iranian persons use EUR 10,000/EUR 40,000 cutoffs — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback)
- Germany: Bundesbank is the competent authority for Iran fund-transfer notifications/authorisations. Its page describes a general ban on transfers between EU and Iranian credit institutions with exemptions for transfers "im Zusammenhang mit Lebensmitteln, Gesundheitsleistungen, medizinischer Ausrüstung" and agricultural/humanitarian purposes; transfers EUR 10,000 to under EUR 40,000 to/from Iranian persons must be notified, and "ab EUR 40.000,00 der Genehmigung" (authorisation) is required. Filing is "von dem Zahlungsverkehrsdienstleister des Auftraggebers bzw. Begünstigten der Zahlung einzureichen". The page shows no update date and does not mention the 2025 snapback — [Deutsche Bundesbank, Iran](https://www.bundesbank.de/de/service/finanzsanktionen/sanktionsregimes/-/iran-610216)
- A German tax-adviser blog (April 2026) claims requirements tightened since autumn 2025 but gives no evidence — [steuerberater-tabak.com](https://steuerberater-tabak.com/alles-ueber-die-meldepflicht-bei-geldtransfers-von-und-nach-iran/) (low reliability)
- HISTORICAL: Arts 30/30a existed in the same structure in 2012-2013 (UK HM Treasury notices) — [HMT notice 26 Mar 2012](https://www.steamshipmutual.com/sites/default/files/downloads/sanctions/HMT%20NOTICE%20DATED%2026%20MARCH%202012%20ON%20EU%20REG%20267.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Birjand farmer is an "Iranian person" (resident in Iran), so payments to him fall under Art 30 (if paid to an Iranian bank) or Art 30a. Payments for saffron/barberries qualify as "transactions regarding foodstuffs". Under EUR 10,000 per transfer: no filing. Splitting a larger purchase into sub-EUR 10,000 transfers to avoid notification would risk being treated as circumvention (Art 41).
- Paying via informal channels (hawala, cash couriers, crypto) or via a Turkish relative to hide the Iranian payee does not remove Art 30/30a obligations for EU-based payers and can engage Art 41 and AML rules.
- Competent authorities are listed in Annex X of 267/2012 and differ by member state (e.g., Germany: Bundesbank for funds transfers, BAFA for export-control licences). Many EU banks refuse Iran-linked payments regardless of legality (commercial de-risking); this is the main practical obstacle.
- HISTORICAL (general knowledge, not verified this session): this transfer regime was lifted on JCPOA Implementation Day (Jan 2016) and only came back with the 2025 snapback.

## 4. EU customs: CN codes, duties, VAT, EORI, importer of record, non-preferential origin, false origin

### Takeaway
Saffron is CN 0910 20 10 (whole, duty 0%) or 0910 20 90 (crushed/ground, 8.5%). Dried barberries most likely fall under the residual CN 0813 40 95 (other dried fruit); the duty rate was not confirmed. Iran gets no tariff preference. Saffron grown in Iran is "wholly obtained" in Iran. Repacking, relabelling or other minimal operations in Turkey do not change its origin. Declaring Turkey or Spain as origin would be a false customs declaration. Since 1 July 2026, low-value e-commerce imports bear a EUR 3 per-item duty.

### Cited Findings
- CN structure: heading 0910 "Ginger, saffron, turmeric (curcuma), thyme, bay leaves, curry and other spices"; 0910 20 10 "Saffron - Neither crushed nor ground"; 0910 20 90 "Crushed or ground". No Iran-specific measure shown — [TARIC Support 0910 20 10](https://www.taricsupport.com/nomenclature/en/0910201000.html)
- Third-country duty: 0910 20 90 (crushed/ground) 8.5%; 0910 20 10 (neither crushed nor ground) 0% — [The Trade Hub EU tariff](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/customs/search?q=09102010); [The Trade Hub classification guide 0910](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/customs/classification-guide/0910) (secondary source; confirm in TARIC/Access2Markets); code confirmed by [UK Trade Info 09102090](https://www.uktradeinfo.com/commodities/09102090)
- Dried fruit: heading 0813 "Fruit, dried, other than that of headings 0801 to 0806; mixtures of nuts or dried fruits of this chapter"; 0813 40 95 10 = goji berries; 0813 40 95 80 = "Other" (residual). No Iran-specific measure listed — [TARIC Support 0813 40 95 80](https://www.taricsupport.com/nomenclature/en/0813409580.html); [TARIC Support 0813 40 95 10](https://www.taricsupport.com/nomenclature/en/0813409510.html)
- The US tariff names barberries explicitly (HTS 0813.40.15.00 "Barberries"); this is a US code and does not settle EU classification — [Wove US tariff](https://tariffs.wove.com/us/tariff/0813.40.15.00/dried-organic-barberries)
- EU Explanatory Notes to the CN mention barberries (Berberis vulgaris), but the excerpt retrieved did not show the heading — [search result referencing EUR-Lex CN Explanatory Notes](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52008XC0530(07))
- Non-preferential origin, Union Customs Code (Reg 952/2013) Art 60(1): "Goods wholly obtained in a single country or territory shall be regarded as having their origin in that country or territory." Art 60(2): goods involving more than one country originate where they "underwent their last, substantial, economically-justified processing or working, in an undertaking equipped for that purpose, resulting in the manufacture of a new product or representing an important stage of manufacture." — [UCC on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2013/952/oj) [EUR-Lex text, not re-fetched this session - verify]
- Delegated Reg 2015/2446 Art 31: wholly obtained goods include "vegetable products harvested there" — [Reg 2015/2446 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2015/2446/oj) [EUR-Lex text, not re-fetched this session - verify]
- Delegated Reg 2015/2446 Art 34 ("minimal operations") lists operations that do not confer origin: preservation during transport/storage (ventilation, spreading out, drying), simple operations (removal of dust, sifting/screening, sorting, washing, cutting up), changes of packing and breaking-up/assembly of consignments, simple placing in bottles, cases, bags or flasks, presentation for sale, affixing marks/labels, disassembly — corroborated by [The Trade Hub summary of Art 34](https://www.thetradehub.eu/es/reglementation/uk/eu/da-2446-2015/article/34) and a 2021 Commission draft noting minimal operations "are not considered as substantial, economically justified processing or working for the purposes of conferring non-preferential origin" — [Mayer Brown 2021](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2021/06/european-commission-opens-consultation-on-draft-regulation-regarding-the-origin-of-goods)
- Fallback where the last operation is minimal (goods not in Annex 22-01): origin goes to the country supplying the major portion of materials, by weight for HS chapters 1-29 — [The Trade Hub Art 34 summary](https://www.thetradehub.eu/es/reglementation/uk/eu/da-2446-2015/article/34)
- Annex 22-01 Chapter 9 rules as reproduced by a third-party site: for tea and cinnamon, "the origin of the goods of this heading shall be the country where the plant grew"; mixing ("the deliberate and proportionally controlled operation consisting in bringing together two or more fungible materials") of Chapter 9 products gives the origin of the country supplying more than half the mixture by weight, and if none does, the country where mixing took place; residual rule: country supplying the largest share of materials by weight — [The Trade Hub origin 0910 (search snippet; page 404 on fetch)](https://www.thetradehub.eu/es/customs/origin/0910)
- Low-value e-commerce: from 1 July 2026 a temporary EUR 3 customs duty applies to low-value parcels (up to EUR 150) imported from outside the EU, per item classification, not per parcel. The Council agreed it 11 Feb 2026. It is temporary (sources say until about July 2028) and pending full removal of the EUR 150 relief in the customs reform — [European Commission, 29 Jun 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ensuring-fairness-and-safety-eur3-customs-duty-low-value-parcels-2026-06-29_en); [VATCalc](https://vatcalc.com/eu/eu-e3-levy-low-value-e-commerce-import-package-july-2026). A separate handling fee (VATCalc says possibly EUR 2 from Nov 2026) is single-source and unconfirmed.

### Inferences
- Saffron grown and harvested in Birjand is wholly obtained in Iran (Art 60(1) UCC + Art 31 DA). Shipping to Turkey and repacking, relabelling, sorting or consolidating there are Art 34 minimal operations, so non-preferential origin stays Iran. If Annex 22-01's "country where the plant grew" logic applies to spices as it does to tea/cinnamon, even grinding in Turkey would not change origin. Under the mixing rule, blending Iranian saffron with a minority of Turkish/Spanish saffron would leave origin as Iran.
- Declaring "Turkey" as origin on an EU import declaration for Iranian saffron would be a false declaration. The declarant is responsible for accuracy (UCC Art 15(2)) and member states must have "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" penalties (UCC Art 42) [EUR-Lex text, not re-fetched]. If the goal is to evade sanctions or food controls, it could also engage sanctions circumvention (Art 41) and food-fraud offences. It would also defeat Reg 2019/1793 controls, which key on origin.
- Turkey route and preferences: the EU-Turkey customs union covers industrial goods; agricultural products move under a separate preferential arrangement that needs Turkish preferential origin proof. Iranian saffron cannot gain Turkish preferential origin by repacking (general knowledge; not verified this session). For whole saffron the duty is 0% anyway, so a Turkish route offers no tariff benefit for whole stigmas.
- Iran is not a WTO member and has no EU preferential agreement or GSP status (general knowledge; not verified). The EU applies its conventional (erga omnes) rates to Iranian goods.
- Importer obligations: the declarant/importer of record needs an EU EORI number (UCC Art 9). Import VAT is due at the member-state rate. Food VAT rates differ widely by member state (reduced rates for foodstuffs vary). Selling on Amazon EU also triggers VAT registration/OSS/IOSS issues; that is outside this note.
- Amazon direct-to-consumer from outside the EU: the per-item EUR 3 duty plus food border controls make small-parcel food imports impractical. Bulk import by a registered EU food business, then fulfilment from inside the EU, is the realistic lawful model.

## 5. EU food law for these imports: FBO duties, registration, official controls (Reg 2017/625, 2019/1793), contaminants (Reg 2023/915), pesticides (Reg 396/2005), labelling (Reg 1169/2011, 2018/775), saffron standards

### Takeaway
Iranian saffron and barberries are not on the EU's increased-checks / emergency list (Reg 2019/1793 as replaced by Reg 2026/194). Iran's only entries there are pistachios and pistachio products. So the goods enter under ordinary risk-based controls. The importer must be a registered food business operator with traceability, must meet aflatoxin/ochratoxin A and pesticide limits, and must label country of origin truthfully. Labelling Iranian saffron as Spanish or with La Mancha imagery is illegal.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Lawful path: an EU-registered FBO (the importer of record) imports in bulk, holds traceability records back to the Birjand grower/exporter, obtains lab certificates (aflatoxins for barberries; OTA; pesticide screen; for saffron, dye adulteration, ideally ISO 3632 grading), and labels "Origin: Iran" (or "Persian saffron"). Home repacking requires registration and hygiene compliance under Reg 852/2004.
- "Saffron" is a dried spice (stigma), so the general OTA limit for dried spices (15 µg/kg) likely applies. The aflatoxin spice row lists named genera only, so no specific EU aflatoxin limit appears to apply to saffron (general safety rules under Reg 178/2002 Art 14 still apply).
- Barberries count as "dried fruits", so B1 2.0 / total 4.0 µg/kg aflatoxin and 2.0 µg/kg OTA apply for retail product.
- Because 2019/1793 and RASFF follow origin, misdeclaring Iranian goods as Turkish can also put the goods under the wrong control regime. That is an extra food-law breach.
- Labelling Iranian saffron "Spanish" (or "envasado en España" presented to imply Spanish origin, or La Mancha imagery) breaches Art 26 of Reg 1169/2011 and possibly PDO protection. The lawful alternative is honest marketing of Iranian/Khorasan provenance. Iran's own GI ("Qaenat saffron", South Khorasan) is not, to my knowledge, registered in the EU; not verified.

## 6. RASFF notifications on Iranian saffron or barberries

### Takeaway
I found no pattern of RASFF alerts for Iranian saffron and none at all for barberries. The one identified recent alert is May 2026: undeclared nut allergen in Iranian saffron, notified by Italy. Iran's recurring RASFF problems are aflatoxins in pistachios. Other Iranian dried fruit (raisins) has also been flagged (MOAH, May 2026).

### Cited Findings

## 7. Market reality: EU (esp. Spain) imports of Iranian saffron; ongoing trade after 2025; Spanish mislabelling and enforcement

### Takeaway
Lawful EU imports of Iranian saffron are large and continuing. Spain imported about 39.6 t of saffron from Iran in 2024 (about 87% of its saffron import volume), and Spanish-government figures cite about USD 61m. Spanish PDO output is tiny (about 349 kg certified in 2024). Spain has prosecuted rings that relabelled Iranian saffron as Spanish/La Mancha (2021 HISTORICAL). No 2024-2026 enforcement case was found.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 (UN Comtrade via WITS): Spain's saffron imports from Iran were USD 50.7m (in thousands: 50,738.68) and 39,649 kg, of total imports 45,351 kg worth USD 56.34m — [World Bank WITS, Spain 091020 imports 2024](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/ESP/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/ALL/product/091020)
