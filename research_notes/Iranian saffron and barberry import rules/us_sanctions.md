# US Sanctions Law on Importing and Selling Iranian-Origin Saffron and Dried Barberries (as of October 2026)

Research date: 18 October 2026. Regulatory text was pulled from the eCFR API (Title 31 "up to date as of 2026-10-15") and from Federal Register and GovInfo primary documents. "Historical" marks facts that no longer describe current law.

Bottom line for the report writer: Iranian-grown saffron and barberries cannot lawfully be imported into or sold in the United States in commercial quantities, whether shipped directly or through Turkey, the UAE or the EU. No general license covers them. A statute (CISADA §103(d)(2)) bars OFAC from licensing commercial imports of Iranian foodstuffs unless the President takes a formal step. Since 10 Sept 2026, OFAC also presumes it will deny all Iran-related license requests. Repacking, grading or blending in a third country does not change the goods' Iranian origin. Relabeling them as Turkish, Afghan or Spanish would be sanctions evasion and customs fraud. US persons, including the Iranian partner if he or she is in the US or holds a green card, are also barred from dealing in these goods anywhere in the world, including sales only in the EU. Since 24 Aug 2026 they also cannot rely on the general license for personal remittances to pay family in Iran.

## 1. What the ITSR (31 CFR Part 560) says about importing and dealing in Iranian-origin goods

### Takeaway
§560.201 bars importing into the US any goods "of Iranian origin". §560.306 defines that term to include anything "grown ... or processed in Iran", so saffron and barberries from Birjand are covered. §560.206 separately bars US persons, wherever they are, from buying, selling, transporting, brokering or financing such goods. §560.208 bars US persons from facilitating such deals by foreigners. §560.203 bars evasion, attempts, causing violations and conspiracies. §560.407 creates a narrow exception for Iranian raw materials that a non-US person substantially transforms or incorporates into a manufactured product in a third country.

### Cited Findings
- **§560.201 (verbatim):** "Except as otherwise authorized pursuant to this part, and notwithstanding any contract entered into or any license or permit granted prior to May 7, 1995, the importation into the United States of any goods or services of Iranian origin or owned or controlled by the Government of Iran, other than information and informational materials within the meaning of section 203(b)(3) of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (50 U.S.C. 1702(b)(3)), is prohibited." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.201](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.201)
- **§560.306(a) (verbatim):** "Except as provided in paragraph (b) of this section, the terms goods of Iranian origin and Iranian-origin goods include: (1) Goods grown, produced, manufactured, extracted, or processed in Iran; and (2) Goods that have entered into Iranian commerce." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.306](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-C/section-560.306)
  - §560.306(b) excludes only goods reexported from Iran under an OFAC authorization, and goods that merely transited Iranian waters or ports. Both exclusions apply only if the goods "were not grown, produced, manufactured, extracted, or processed in Iran". Neither helps Iranian-grown crops. — [eCFR 560.306](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-C/section-560.306)
- **§560.206 (verbatim):** "(a) Except as otherwise authorized pursuant to this part, and notwithstanding any contract entered into or any license or permit granted prior to May 7, 1995, no United States person, wherever located, may engage in any transaction or dealing in or related to: (1) Goods or services of Iranian origin or owned or controlled by the Government of Iran; or (2) Goods, technology, or services for exportation, reexportation, sale or supply, directly or indirectly, to Iran or the Government of Iran. (b) For purposes of paragraph (a) of this section, the term transaction or dealing includes but is not limited to purchasing, selling, transporting, swapping, brokering, approving, financing, facilitating, or guaranteeing." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.206](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.206)
- **§560.208 (verbatim):** "...no United States person, wherever located, may approve, finance, facilitate, or guarantee any transaction by a foreign person where the transaction by that foreign person would be prohibited by this part if performed by a United States person or within the United States." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.208](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.208)
- **§560.203 (verbatim):** "(a) Any transaction on or after the effective date that evades or avoids, has the purpose of evading or avoiding, causes a violation of, or attempts to violate any of the prohibitions set forth in this part is prohibited. (b) Any conspiracy formed to violate any of the prohibitions set forth in this part is prohibited." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.203](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.203)
- **§560.204 (excerpt):** it prohibits "the exportation, reexportation, sale, or supply, directly or indirectly, from the United States, or by a United States person, wherever located, of any goods, technology, or services to Iran or the Government of Iran". This covers financial services. Under §560.427(a)(1), it includes "The transfer of funds, directly or indirectly, from the United States or by a U.S. person, wherever located, to Iran". — [eCFR 560.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.204); [eCFR 560.427](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.427)
- **§560.407 (verbatim):** "(a) Importation into the United States from third countries of goods containing Iranian-origin raw materials or components and transactions relating to such goods are not prohibited by § 560.201 or § 560.206 if those raw materials or components have been incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country by a person other than a United States person. (b) Transactions relating to Iranian-origin goods that have not been incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country are prohibited." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.407](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.407)
- **§560.314 (verbatim):** "The term United States person or U.S. person means any United States citizen, permanent resident alien, entity organized under the laws of the United States or any jurisdiction within the United States (including foreign branches), or any person in the United States." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.314](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-C/section-560.314)
- **§560.215(a) (verbatim):** "...an entity that is owned or controlled by a United States person and established or maintained outside the United States is prohibited from knowingly engaging in any transaction, directly or indirectly, with the Government of Iran or any person subject to the jurisdiction of the Government of Iran that would be prohibited pursuant to this part if engaged in by a United States person or in the United States." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.215](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.215)
- **§560.105(c), detention and seizure (verbatim):** "Import shipments into the United States of Iranian-origin goods in violation of § 560.201 ... shall be detained. No such import, export, or reexport will be permitted to proceed, except as specifically authorized by or on behalf of the Secretary of the Treasury. Unless licensed, such shipments are subject to penalty or seizure and forfeiture action, under the customs laws or other applicable provisions of law, depending on the circumstances." This text was moved into §560.105 on 30 Sept 2026; it was formerly §560.702. — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.105](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-A/section-560.105); [91 FR 61727 (30 Sept 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20007/removing-duplicative-penalties-information-and-reorganizing-certain-parts)
- **Statutory overlay, CISADA §103(b)(1)(A) (22 U.S.C. 8512) (verbatim):** "Except as provided in subparagraph (B), no good or service of Iranian origin may be imported directly or indirectly into the United States." The only exceptions are those in IEEPA §203(b), such as informational materials. — [22 U.S.C. 8512 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/8512)

### Inferences
- Saffron and barberries grown in Birjand are "goods grown ... in Iran" under §560.306(a)(1). They stay Iranian-origin wherever they are later shipped, stored or repacked.
- §560.201 applies to the act of importing into the US, whoever does it. A non-US seller in the EU who ships to US customers, or into Amazon US warehouses, is making or causing prohibited imports. "Causing" is prohibited by §560.203 and by 50 U.S.C. 1705(a).
- If the Iranian-national partner lives in the US or holds a green card, he or she is a US person under §560.314. §560.206 then reaches his or her dealings worldwide.

### Gaps
- No OFAC FAQ was found that applies §560.407 specifically to spices or dried fruit.

## 2. History: the 2000 easing, the 2010 revocation, the 2016–2018 JCPOA window, and the current licensing position

### Takeaway
The legal status of Iranian foodstuffs has changed three times (historical). It opened in April 2000, closed on 29 Sept 2010 under CISADA, reopened on 16 Jan 2016 under the JCPOA, and closed again after a wind-down ending 6 Aug 2018. In October 2026 there is no general license for Iranian foodstuffs, saffron or dried fruit. CISADA §103(d)(2) bars any exception for commercial imports of these foods unless the President issues a regulation and certifies to Congress. On 10 Sept 2026 OFAC adopted a presumption of denial for all Iran-related license requests. A specific license for commercial sale is therefore not a realistic path.

### Cited Findings
- **2000 easing (historical):** OFAC's rule, effective 28 April 2000, added §560.534 "authoriz[ing] the importation into the United States of Iranian-origin foodstuffs intended for human consumption that are classified under chapters 2-23 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule". It also covered carpets under chapter 57 and heading 9706.00.0060. It followed Secretary Albright's 17 March 2000 announcement that sanctions would be eased "to allow Americans to purchase and import carpets and food products such as dried fruits, nuts, and caviar from Iran." — [65 FR (3 May 2000), FR Doc 00-11009](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2000/05/03/00-11009/iranian-transactions-regulations-licensing-of-imports-of-and-dealings-in-certain-iranian-origin)
- **2010 revocation (historical):** A rule effective 29 Sept 2010 removed the general licenses for imports of and dealings in Iranian-origin foodstuffs and carpets. It also implemented CISADA §103's import ban. OFAC stated: "as of September 29, 2010 ... sections 560.534 and 560.535 of the ITR will be revoked, and OFAC will no longer authorize, by general or specific license, the commercial importation into the United States of these foodstuffs and carpets of Iranian- origin." — [75 FR 59611 (28 Sept 2010)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/09/28/2010-24211/iranian-transactions-regulations)
  - The same notice said non-commercial imports of these foods "would also be prohibited by section 560.201 of the ITR, unless otherwise authorized or exempt". It pointed to the household-goods general license in §560.524(b). — [75 FR 59611](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/09/28/2010-24211/iranian-transactions-regulations)
- **CISADA §103(d)(2) (22 U.S.C. 8512(d)(2)), current statute (verbatim):** "No exception to the prohibition under subsection (b)(1) may be made for the commercial importation of an Iranian origin good described in section 560.534(a) of title 31, Code of Federal Regulations (as in effect on the day before July 1, 2010), unless the President— (A) prescribes a regulation providing for such an exception on or after July 1, 2010; and (B) submits to the appropriate congressional committees— (i) a certification in writing that the exception is in the national interest of the United States; and (ii) a report describing the reasons for the exception." — [22 U.S.C. 8512 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/8512)
- **2016 JCPOA reopening (historical):** Under the JCPOA, OFAC re-added §560.534 "to authorize by general license the importation into the United States of, and dealings in, certain Iranian-origin foodstuffs and carpets from Iran or a third country". It used the CISADA §103(d)(2) route: the rule was published and the Secretary of State certified the exception to Congress. — [81 FR 3330 (21 Jan 2016)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2016-01-21/html/2016-01227.htm)
- **2018 re-closure (historical):** After NSPM-11 ended US participation in the JCPOA, OFAC narrowed §560.534 to a wind-down of "The importation into the United States, from Iran or a third country, of ... (i) Foodstuffs intended for human consumption that are classified under chapters 2-23 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule". The wind-down ran "through 11:59 p.m. eastern daylight time on August 6, 2018". The same window applied to letters of credit and brokering under §560.535. — [83 FR 30335 (28 June 2018)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/06/28/2018-13939/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
- **Current text:** §560.534 and §560.535 remain in the eCFR only as expired wind-down provisions ("authorized through 11:59 p.m. eastern daylight time on August 6, 2018"). — [eCFR 560.534](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-E/section-560.534); [eCFR 560.535](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-E/section-560.535)
- **Licensing policy since 10 Sept 2026 (verbatim, OFAC Statement of Licensing Policy):** "the following Statement of Licensing Policy establishes a presumed denial for licensing requests to engage in activities which are prohibited by the Iranian Transactions and Sanctions Regulations (ITSR), 31 C.F.R. part 560, and other Iran-related authorities ... Under this Statement of Licensing Policy, Iran-related specific licenses may only be issued as required by law or in exceptional and urgent circumstances, such as risk to life, limb, or environmental safety." The policy also suspends earlier licensing policies that "had provided a favorable licensing posture for certain activities." It was signed by OFAC Director Bradley T. Smith on 10 Sept 2026. — [OFAC Statement of Licensing Policy on Iran-Related Requests (10 Sept 2026)](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/936916/download?inline); announced in [Treasury GovDelivery bulletin, 10 Sept 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USTREAS/bulletins/4298920)
- **2026 general licenses:** The Iran general licenses issued in 2026 covered other matters only: GL U (20 March 2026) and GL X/X1 (June–July 2026) for Iranian crude and petroleum cargoes, and wind-down GLs T, V, Y, Z, AA, BB, CC and DD. None covers foodstuffs. — [91 FR 35141](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11614/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-u-and-v); [91 FR 61758](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20010/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-x-and-x1); [91 FR 57795](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18576/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-cc-and-dd); [91 FR 55265](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/27/2026-17491/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-aa-and-bb)

### Inferences
- Saffron is spice heading 0910 (HTS chapter 9) and dried barberries are dried fruit (chapter 8). Both fall within "chapters 2-23", so they sit squarely within the CISADA §103(d)(2) bar. The HTS headings are from general tariff knowledge and were not verified against the USITC HTS in this research.
- A specific license for commercial import or sale of Iranian saffron or barberries is effectively unavailable. CISADA bars it absent presidential action, and OFAC's 10 Sept 2026 policy presumes denial. Applying is not a realistic business path in October 2026.
- Any reopening would need a new OFAC regulation plus a Secretary of State certification to Congress, as in January 2016. Nothing in the record suggests that is in prospect.

### Gaps
- The full Federal Register citation page for the 2000 rule (65 FR page) was not captured. It was published 3 May 2000 as FR Doc 00-11009 and was effective 28 April 2000.

## 3. Indirect importation through third countries (Turkey, UAE, EU)

### Takeaway
Routing through Turkey or another third country does not make the import lawful. CISADA bans Iranian-origin goods imported "directly or indirectly" into the US. OFAC's own guidance says such goods may not be imported "either directly or through third countries". §560.203 makes any arrangement that evades or tries to evade the ban a separate violation. Shipping Birjand saffron to relatives in Turkey and then on to US buyers would be a prohibited indirect import. Relabeling it as Turkish would add evasion and customs-fraud exposure.

### Cited Findings
- CISADA §103(b)(1)(A): "no good or service of Iranian origin may be imported directly or indirectly into the United States." — [22 U.S.C. 8512 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/8512)
- OFAC's Iran overview brochure (historical document, c. 2010–2012, still consistent with current rules): "IMPORTS FROM IRAN - Goods or services of Iranian origin may not be imported into the United States, either directly or through third countries, with the following exceptions: a) Gifts valued at $100 or less; b) Information and informational materials; c) Household and personal effects ... and d) Accompanied baggage for personal use normally incident to travel." — [OFAC, "An overview of O.F.A.C. Regulations involving Sanctions against Iran"](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/iran.pdf)
- The same brochure on US persons abroad: "U.S. persons may not import such goods or services into or export them from foreign locations." — [OFAC Iran overview](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/iran.pdf)
- §560.407(b): "Transactions relating to Iranian-origin goods that have not been incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country are prohibited." — [eCFR 560.407](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.407)
- The 2018 wind-down rule described the formerly permitted trade as importation "from Iran or a third country". This confirms that OFAC treats imports via third countries as within the import rules. — [83 FR 30335](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/06/28/2018-13939/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
- OFAC in 2010: goods that pass through Iran become Iranian-origin. "foodstuffs and carpets of third-country origin that are transshipped through Iran become goods of Iranian-origin." The reverse does not apply: transshipping through a third country does not remove Iranian origin. — [75 FR 59611](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/09/28/2010-24211/iranian-transactions-regulations)
- **Turkey-specific 2026 risk:** On 4 Sept 2026 OFAC blocked the Turkish banks "Golden Global Yatirim Bankasi Anonim Sirketi" and "Golden Global Varlik Kiralama Anonim Sirketi" under E.O. 13902. GL CC allowed wind-down only through 19 Sept 2026. — [91 FR 57795 (GL CC)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18576/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-cc-and-dd)
- **Operation Economic Outcast (24 Aug 2026):** Treasury said countries hosting Iran-related networks "will be given a defined timeline to shut down the Iran-related activity we have identified ... If they fail to act, Treasury will act." The release names UAE-based networks. — [Treasury press release sb0613](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0613)

### Inferences
- Iran → Turkey (relatives) → US customers or Amazon US is a textbook prohibited indirect import under §560.201 and CISADA §103(b)(1). Arranging it to hide the origin is evasion under §560.203. Each participant who knowingly arranges it could face conspiracy liability: the sellers, the relatives in Turkey and any US-person helper.
- Iran → Turkey → EU buyers involves no US import, so §560.201 does not apply. But §560.206 and §560.208 still bind any US person who takes part. Separate EU and Turkish rules apply and are outside this note's scope.

### Gaps
- No current OFAC FAQ was found that uses the word "transshipment" for imports of Iranian-origin consumer goods. The brochure language above is the clearest OFAC statement found, and it is older guidance.

## 4. Third-country processing, packaging, blending and country-of-origin rules (OFAC and CBP)

### Takeaway
§560.407 frees Iranian inputs only if a non-US person incorporates them into a "manufactured product" or "substantially transforms" them in a third country. Re-sorting, grading, drying, repacking, branding or blending saffron threads or barberries does not create a new article. CBP precedent on nuts says even roasting, salting and blending do not substantially transform them. These steps in Turkey would leave the goods Iranian-origin for both OFAC and CBP. A US person cannot do the transforming in any case. No CBP ruling or enforcement action specific to Iranian saffron or barberries was found.

### Cited Findings
- §560.407(a) requires that Iranian raw materials be "incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country by a person other than a United States person". — [eCFR 560.407](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.407)
- **CBP and nuts:** Treasury Decision 85-158 (1985) held that roasting, salting and blending pistachios did not substantially transform them. CBP New York Ruling I83434 held that raw nuts, including pistachios, dry-roasted in Hong Kong "are not substantially transformed by that processing" and that "their country of origin, for marking purposes, will be the country that produced the raw nut." These are reported from search results, and CBP's ruling page was not fetched in full. — [CBP CROSS ruling I83434](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/I83434); background in [American Pistachio Growers, Historical Accomplishments](https://americanpistachios.org/about-us/grower-resources/historical-accomplishments)
- The American Pistachio Growers account says that before the 1985 petition, CBP had treated roasting as a substantial transformation. That would have let Iranian pistachios roasted in the US be labeled "Product of U.S." The rule was changed and upheld by the Court of International Trade. This is an advocacy source. — [American Pistachio Growers](https://americanpistachios.org/about-us/grower-resources/historical-accomplishments)
- CBP's general test for substantial transformation asks whether a new article of commerce emerges with a new name, character or use. Packaging alone is typically not enough. — [Amundsen Davis client alert](https://www.amundsendavislaw.com/alert-how-to-understand-substantial-transformation-in-a-country-of-origin-determination)
- **OFAC enforcement on mislabeled third-country trade in Iranian goods:** In Sojitz (Hong Kong) (11 Jan 2022), the company paid $5,228,298. Employees bought about 64,000 tons of Iranian-origin HDPE from a Thai supplier, paid through US banks in US dollars ($75,603,411 over 60 transfers), and asked the supplier to leave Iran out of shipping documents. — [OFAC recent actions 20220111](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20220111_33); [Kelley Drye summary](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/trade-and-manufacturing-monitor/hong-kong-company-pays-5m-fine-after-employees-buy-and-sell-iranian-origin-goods-using-u-s-dollars)
- **Non-US mislabeling of Iranian saffron:** In 2021 Spanish police arrested 17 people in a ring accused of importing Iranian saffron and passing it off as Spanish. This is a Spanish case, not a US one. — [Washington Post, 7 May 2021](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2021/05/07/saffron-gang-fraud/)
- **Customs-fraud statutes that apply to false origin declarations:**
  - 18 U.S.C. 542 covers entering goods "by means of any fraudulent or false invoice, declaration ... or by means of any false statement". Penalty: "fined for each offense under this title or imprisoned not more than two years, or both". — [18 U.S.C. 542 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/542)
  - 18 U.S.C. 545 covers anyone who "fraudulently or knowingly imports or brings into the United States, any merchandise contrary to law, or receives, conceals, buys, sells, or in any manner facilitates the transportation, concealment, or sale of such merchandise after importation". Penalty: "fined under this title or imprisoned not more than 20 years, or both". — [18 U.S.C. 545 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/545)

### Inferences
- Under OFAC's §560.407, packing saffron into retail tins, blending grades or adding Turkish-made packaging does not turn it into a "manufactured product". It also fails CBP's new name, character or use test. The goods remain Iranian-origin.
- Using Iranian saffron as a minor ingredient in a genuinely new product made by a non-US person in Turkey might qualify under §560.407(a). Examples would be a saffron-flavored confection or a prepared rice mix. This is a fact-specific grey area: OFAC has no saffron guidance, and §560.203 bars schemes whose purpose is evasion. A US person could not lawfully set up or direct such a scheme because of §560.206 and §560.208. Sellers should treat it as high-risk and get a written legal opinion.
- Declaring Iranian saffron as Turkish, Afghan or Spanish at US entry would breach OFAC rules and CBP marking rules, and could support 18 U.S.C. 542 and 545 charges and seizure or forfeiture under §560.105(c).

### Gaps
- No CBP CROSS ruling was found on the country of origin of saffron or barberries. No US case or CBP seizure involving mislabeled Iranian saffron or pistachios was found. I found no reliable source on US seizures of Iranian saffron specifically.

## 5. Enforcement examples and penalty amounts

### Takeaway
- **Civil (IEEPA, now consolidated in 31 CFR Part 505):** up to the greater of $377,700 per violation or twice the transaction value.
- **Criminal:** up to $1,000,000 and 20 years' imprisonment for willful violations.
- **Limitation period:** 10 years for civil and criminal actions.
- **Seizure:** goods are detained and may be forfeited.

No US case on Iranian saffron or barberries was found. OFAC has penalized Iranian-goods imports and individual Iranian-Americans, most recently a $1.43 million settlement with an individual on 10 Sept 2026. Treasury says dual nationals and people with family ties to Iran are an enforcement focus under Operation Economic Outcast.

### Cited Findings
- **Civil maximum (verbatim, new 31 CFR Part 505, effective 25 Sept 2026):** "IEEPA provides for a maximum civil penalty not to exceed the greater of $377,700 or an amount that is twice the amount of the transaction that is the basis of the violation with respect to which the penalty is imposed." — [91 FR 60821 (25 Sept 2026), Sanctions Penalties Regulations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/25/2026-19678/sanctions-penalties-regulations)
- **Criminal maximum (verbatim, §505.401):** a person who "willfully commits, willfully attempts to commit, willfully conspires to commit, or aids or abets in the commission of a violation ... may, upon conviction, be fined not more than $1,000,000, or if a natural person, be imprisoned for not more than 20 years, or both." — [91 FR 60821](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/25/2026-19678/sanctions-penalties-regulations); statute at [50 U.S.C. 1705 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/1705)
- **Unlawful acts (50 U.S.C. 1705(a)):** "It shall be unlawful for a person to violate, attempt to violate, conspire to violate, or cause a violation of any license, order, regulation, or prohibition issued under this chapter." — [50 U.S.C. 1705](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/1705)
- **Limitation period (50 U.S.C. 1705(d), as amended Apr. 24, 2024):** civil actions must be "commenced within 10 years after the latest date of the violation", and an indictment must be found "within 10 years". — [50 U.S.C. 1705](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/1705)
- **Regulatory reorganization:** On 30 Sept 2026 OFAC removed §560.701 and the other Part 560 penalty sections. §560.105(a) now points to "part 505, subpart D". — [91 FR 61727](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20007/removing-duplicative-penalties-information-and-reorganizing-certain-parts); [eCFR 560.105](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-A/section-560.105)
- **OFAC individual settlement, 10 Sept 2026:** "A natural U.S. person ... agreed to pay $1,427,230 to settle their potential civil liability for 39 apparent violations of OFAC sanctions on Iran." The conduct included consulting for an Iranian software company, receiving "Iranian-origin dividends to their U.S. bank accounts", and acquiring "real property in Iran". OFAC found the violations "not voluntarily self-disclosed and were egregious", and the FBI took part. — [OFAC Enforcement Release, 10 Sept 2026](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/936911/download?inline)
  - Foley's reading is that dual nationals and people with family or investment ties to Iran are within the enforcement focus. This is law-firm commentary. — [Foley & Lardner, Sept 2026 roundup](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/10/september-2026-u-s-sanctions-roundup-operation-economic-outcast-intensifies-against-iran-cuba-rules-tighten-and-ofac-overhauls-its-regulations/)
- **Iranian goods import penalty (historical):** "Mahdavi's A&A Rug Company, Norcross, Georgia has remitted $9,240.00 to settle allegations of violations of the Iranian Transactions Regulations occurring on July 17, 2003. OFAC alleged that Mahdavi's A&A Rug Company imported Iranian goods in violation of § 560.201". The release does not say the goods were carpets. — [OFAC enforcement information (c. 2006)](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/12946/download)
- **Sojitz (Hong Kong), 2022:** $5,228,298 for dealing in Iranian-origin HDPE bought through a Thai intermediary and paid in US dollars. This shows that non-US persons who route payments for Iranian-origin goods through US banks cause violations. — [OFAC recent actions 20220111](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20220111_33); [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/asia/sojitz-fined-by-us-over-rogue-employees-iran-trade-transactions/)
- **Turkey/UAE transshipment prosecution (export side, analog):** Alabama man Ray Hunt pleaded guilty in July 2024 to conspiring to export US goods to Iran through transshipment companies in Turkey and the UAE, with payments routed through UAE banks. He was sentenced to five years in prison. — [DOJ OPA, guilty plea](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-man-pleads-guilty-violating-iran-sanctions); [DOJ OPA, sentencing](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-man-sentenced-five-years-prison-violating-us-sanctions-iran)
- **Pistachio-related prosecution (money transfer, not goods):** According to Iran International, Ali Amin, linked to a pistachio family business, pleaded guilty to illegally moving $17 million from Iran to the US through unlicensed means. The court record was not verified. — [Iran International, Aug 2023](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202308207265)

### Inferences
- For a small saffron business, the realistic exposures are:
  - CBP detention and forfeiture of shipments.
  - OFAC civil penalties scaled to transaction value. Even small sales can draw penalties of $377,700 per violation, because the cap is the greater of $377,700 or twice the transaction value.
  - Criminal prosecution if they act willfully, for example by knowingly relabeling goods as Turkish.
  - Bank account closures and Amazon account suspension, which are commercial consequences rather than legal penalties.
- OFAC's 2026 focus on individuals with family ties to Iran raises the odds that a small dual-national operation would be noticed. This is commentary-based, not a statistic.

### Gaps
- No DOJ prosecution, OFAC penalty or CBP seizure involving Iranian saffron, barberries or dried fruit was found. Searches of justice.gov and OFAC enforcement material returned none. The finding here is that none was found, not that none exists.
- No 2026 inflation adjustment to the $377,700 figure was found. Part 505, published 25 Sept 2026, still states $377,700.

## 6. Exemptions and general licenses: gifts, baggage, household effects, informational materials

### Takeaway
Narrow personal-use exemptions exist: gifts worth $100 or less, accompanied baggage for personal use, used household effects of arriving persons, and informational materials. Each one excludes goods for sale or is non-commercial by its terms. None permits commercial sale of saffron or barberries in the US, on Amazon or otherwise. Splitting shipments into many gifts of $100 or less to sell them would be evasion under §560.203.

### Cited Findings
- **Gifts, §560.506 (verbatim):** "The importation into the United States of Iranian-origin goods from Iran or a third country ... [is] authorized for goods sent as gifts to persons provided that the value of each gift is not more than $100; the goods are of a type and in quantities normally given as gifts between individuals; and the goods are not controlled for chemical and biological weapons (CB), missile technology (MT), national security (NS), or nuclear proliferation (NP)." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.506](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-E/section-560.506)
- **Household and personal effects, §560.524(b) (verbatim):** "The importation of Iranian-origin household and personal effects, including baggage and articles for family use, of persons arriving in the United States is authorized; to qualify, articles included in such effects must have been actually used abroad by such persons or by other family members from the same foreign household, must not be intended for any other person or for sale, and must not be otherwise prohibited from importation." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.524](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-E/section-560.524)
- **Travel and accompanied baggage, §560.210(d) (verbatim):** "The prohibitions contained in this part do not apply to transactions ordinarily incident to travel to or from any country, including importation or exportation of accompanied baggage for personal use..." — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.210](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.210)
- **Informational materials, §560.210(c):** this covers publications, films, artworks and similar items defined in §560.315, not food. — [eCFR 560.210](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.210); [eCFR 560.315](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-C/section-560.315)
- **New §560.210(g), 30 Sept 2026:** "The prohibitions in § 560.211(c)(1)(iii) do not apply with respect to any person for conducting or facilitating a transaction for the provision (including any sale) of agricultural commodities, food, medicine, or medical devices to Iran." This exempts only E.O. 13902 blocking for food going TO Iran. It does not touch the §560.201 import ban. — [91 FR 61759 (30 Sept 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-19978/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
- **2010 note:** with §560.534 revoked, even non-commercial imports of Iranian foods became prohibited "unless otherwise authorized or exempt". — [75 FR 59611](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/09/28/2010-24211/iranian-transactions-regulations)

### Inferences
- A traveler may bring a reasonable amount of Iranian saffron in accompanied baggage for personal use. A relative may mail a bona fide gift worth $100 or less to a US individual. Neither route can be used to stock an Amazon listing or to resell.
- A pattern of many gifts of $100 or less sent to the sellers or to their customers would fail the "normally given as gifts between individuals" test. It would also be evasion under §560.203.

### Gaps
- OFAC has published no guidance on how it values a saffron gift, for example at retail or wholesale price. None was found.

## 7. US-person issues if the sellers are US persons but sell only in the EU, and non-US persons selling into the US

### Takeaway
A US person (citizen, green-card holder or anyone physically in the US) may not buy, sell, transport, broker, finance or facilitate Iranian-origin saffron or barberries anywhere in the world, including sales only in the EU (§560.206 and §560.208). A non-US company owned or controlled by that US person is barred too (§560.215). Paying the Birjand relative is a prohibited export of financial services under §560.204 and §560.427. The general license for noncommercial personal remittances (§560.550) has been suspended since 24 Aug 2026. A non-US seller in the EU with no US nexus is outside §560.206 for EU-only sales. That seller still may not sell into the US, use US-dollar clearing or US banks, or involve US-person partners or platforms.

### Cited Findings
- §560.206(a)(1) applies to "no United States person, wherever located" for "any transaction or dealing in or related to: (1) Goods or services of Iranian origin". Under (b), "transaction or dealing" includes "purchasing, selling, transporting, swapping, brokering, approving, financing, facilitating, or guaranteeing". — [eCFR 560.206](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.206)
- §560.417 lists examples of prohibited facilitation. One is when a US person "(b) Refers to a foreign person purchase orders, requests for bids, or similar business opportunities involving Iran or the Government of Iran to which the United States person could not directly respond as a result of the prohibitions contained in this part". — [eCFR 31 CFR 560.417](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.417)
- §560.215(a) bars a foreign entity "owned or controlled by a United States person" from transactions with persons "subject to the jurisdiction of the Government of Iran" that would be prohibited for a US person. Under §560.105(b), the parent US person can be penalized for the entity's violations. — [eCFR 560.215](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-B/section-560.215); [eCFR 560.105](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-A/section-560.105)
- §560.427(a)(1): the §560.204 ban on exporting financial services covers "The transfer of funds, directly or indirectly, from the United States or by a U.S. person, wherever located, to Iran or the Government of Iran". — [eCFR 560.427](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-D/section-560.427)
- **Remittance general license suspended:** Effective 24 Aug 2026, OFAC "indefinitely suspend[ed] the general licenses contained at 31 CFR 560.544, 560.550, and 560.554 ... certain noncommercial, personal remittances to or from Iran ... As a result of this suspension, any such transactions are no longer authorized by OFAC as of August 24, 2026." GL BB allowed wind-down until 8 Sept 2026. — [91 FR 54957 (26 Aug 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17426/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations); [91 FR 55265 (GL BB)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/27/2026-17491/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-aa-and-bb)
  - The eCFR now reads "§ 560.550 was stayed indefinitely." — [eCFR Part 560](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560)
  - Even before the suspension, §560.550 covered only noncommercial personal remittances. Payment for goods was never covered.
- **Non-US persons and the US financial system:** In the Sojitz HK case, a non-US company was penalized because its US-dollar payments for Iranian-origin goods were processed by US financial institutions. — [OFAC recent actions 20220111](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20220111_33)
- **OFAC brochure:** "a U.S. person, wherever located, or any person acting within the United States, may not broker offshore transactions that benefit Iran or the Government of Iran, including sales of foreign goods or arranging for third-country financing or guarantees." — [OFAC Iran overview](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/iran.pdf)

### Inferences
Scenario matrix for the report writer:

- **(a) Both partners are US persons, selling into the US.** Prohibited under §560.201 and §560.206. Not licensable in practice.
- **(b) Both are US persons, selling only in the EU.** Still prohibited under §560.206, which reaches US persons wherever located. The same applies to any EU company they own or control (§560.215). Paying the Birjand farmer is also prohibited under §560.204 and §560.427.
- **(c) One partner is a US person and the other a non-US person in the EU, selling only in the EU.** The US-person partner may not take part, invest, manage, advise, finance or refer business (§560.206, §560.208, §560.417). The non-US partner's EU-only sales are not reached by the ITSR if there is no US nexus. A US nexus would include USD payments, US banks, US-based platforms or services, or US-person involvement. EU law must be checked separately.
- **(d) Non-US persons in the EU selling into the US, including via Amazon.com or Amazon US FBA.** Prohibited: they would import or cause imports of Iranian-origin goods (§560.201, §560.203, 50 U.S.C. 1705(a)). Amazon and US carriers and banks would be dealing in the goods, and causing a US person to violate the rules is itself a violation. Exposure includes OFAC civil penalties, criminal charges if willful, seizure, and possible designation risk under the 2026 Iran campaign.
- **US-based marketplaces, payment processors and banks.** These are US persons barred by §560.206 from dealing in Iranian-origin goods. Using them for Iranian saffron sales, even sales to EU customers, risks causing their violations.

### Gaps
- The partners' citizenship and residency are unknown, so the matrix above is conditional.
- No OFAC FAQ specifically on Amazon or other marketplace sales of Iranian-origin goods was found.

## 8. 2025–2026 changes (NSPM-2, Operation Economic Outcast, new GLs and suspensions)

### Takeaway
Since February 2025 US policy has moved toward maximum pressure. It intensified sharply in August to October 2026. During 2026 OFAC briefly issued general licenses for Iranian oil cargoes (March and June), then revoked them. It launched "Operation Economic Outcast" on 24 Aug 2026, suspended remittance, educational and aviation general licenses, and expanded E.O. 13902 sector sanctions. On 10 Sept 2026 it adopted a presumption of denial for licenses. No relaxation affecting Iranian agricultural or food imports was found. The trend is tightening.

### Cited Findings
- **NSPM-2 (4 Feb 2025)** restored "maximum pressure". It directed Treasury to impose sanctions or enforcement remedies against those violating Iran sanctions. — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/president-trump-renews-maximum-pressure-campaign-on-iran.html); [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/maximum-pressure-on-iran-is-back-what-this-means-for-sanctions-and-export-controls); [Iran Watch, NSPM-2](https://www.iranwatch.org/node/13345)
- **GL U (20 March 2026)** authorized delivery and sale of Iranian crude and petroleum products loaded by that date, through 19 April 2026. **GL X (21 June 2026)** authorized production, delivery and sale of Iranian crude, petrochemicals and petroleum products through 21 Aug 2026. GL X was revoked and replaced by wind-down GL X1 effective 7 July 2026. Neither touched foodstuffs. — [91 FR 35141](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11614/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-u-and-v); [91 FR 61758](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20010/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-x-and-x1)
- **Operation Economic Outcast (24 Aug 2026):** Treasury called it "an unprecedented, whole-of-government, economic campaign". Secretary Bessent said it aims to "sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone." It sanctioned nearly 60 targets, designated five sectors, and suspended remittance and academic general licenses. — [Treasury press release sb0613](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0613); the launch date is confirmed in [OFAC Statement of Licensing Policy](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/936916/download?inline)
- **Sector determinations under E.O. 13902 §1(a)(i):**
  - 24 Aug 2026: aviation, digital asset, gold, shipping and technology sectors. — [91 FR 55265](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/27/2026-17487/publication-of-a-determination-issued-pursuant-to-executive-order-13902)
  - 1 Oct 2026: automotive and rail sectors. — [91 FR 64758](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20839/publication-of-an-iran-related-determination)
  - Agriculture and food have not been designated as sectors.
- **General licenses suspended:**
  - 24 Aug 2026: §560.544, §560.550 (personal remittances), §560.554, and GLs F and G. — [91 FR 54957](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17426/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
  - 8 Sept 2026: §560.522, §560.528 and §560.529, and GL J-1. OFAC gave as reasons "Iran's continued disruptions to global energy markets, attacks on partners and allies in the Middle East, reconstitution of its conventional and nuclear weapons programs, efforts to monetize the Strait of Hormuz, and continued support to terrorist proxies". — [91 FR 57511 (10 Sept 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/10/2026-18461/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
- **Presumption of denial for Iran licenses (10 Sept 2026):** see Section 2. — [OFAC SLP](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/936916/download?inline)
- **30 Sept 2026 ITSR amendment:** it incorporated E.O. 13902 blocking into §560.211(c)(1)(iii), revised the definition of "Iran" in §560.303, and added exemptions in §560.210(g) (food, medicine and similar items TO Iran) and §560.210(h) (UN official business). — [91 FR 61759](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-19978/iranian-transactions-and-sanctions-regulations)
- **Penalty consolidation:** new 31 CFR Part 505 effective 25 Sept 2026, with Part 560 penalty sections removed on 30 Sept 2026. — [91 FR 60821](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/25/2026-19678/sanctions-penalties-regulations); [91 FR 61727](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20007/removing-duplicative-penalties-information-and-reorganizing-certain-parts)
- **Turkish bank designations (4 Sept 2026):** Golden Global Yatirim Bankasi A.S. and Golden Global Varlik Kiralama A.S. — [91 FR 57795](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18576/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-cc-and-dd)

### Inferences
- The direction of travel is toward tighter enforcement against Iran-linked commerce, including by individuals with family ties to Iran. A 2016-style reopening for Iranian foodstuffs is very unlikely in the near term. It would need a policy reversal plus a CISADA §103(d)(2) certification.
- Routing payments or goods through Turkish or UAE intermediaries now carries extra risk. Those jurisdictions are named targets of the 2026 campaign, and intermediaries there could themselves be designated.

### Gaps
- No OFAC FAQ updates issued in 2026 specifically on Iranian-origin consumer goods were found.
- The full list of persons designated in August to October 2026 was not reviewed for saffron, nut or dried-fruit exporters, for example in Khorasan.
