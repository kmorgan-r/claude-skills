## 1. Iranian export rules: who may export, saffron-specific rules, standards, currency repatriation, barberry rules

### Takeaway
Under Iranian law, commercial export requires a commercial card (kart-e bazargani). ICCIMA issues the card and the Ministry of Commerce approves it. A small farmer without a card must sell through, or export under, a card-holding exporter or company. Saffron has a long history of rules on bulk versus packaged exports and on government-set export prices, but this research found no confirmed 2026 bulk-export ban or minimum-price decree. Rules on repatriating export currency (rafe' ta'ahod-e arzi) are in flux in 2026: deadlines have been extended, the government allows "import-in-exchange-for-export", and there are unofficial reports that crypto settlement is allowed.

### Cited Findings
**Commercial card and who may export**
- Iran's Export-Import Regulation Act requires a commercial card for "engaging in the business of exportation and importation of goods for commercial purposes". The Iran Chamber of Commerce, Industries and Mines issues the card and the Ministry of Commerce approves it — [Export-Import Regulations Act (English text, hosted by Russia's FSVPS)](https://old.fsvps.gov.ru/fsvps-docs/ru/importExport/iran/files/iran_law_import_export.pdf)
- The Act's notes exempt some groups from the card: cooperatives of frontier-zone inhabitants, Iranian mariners, hawkers and workers residing abroad. Farmers' cooperatives are not named among the exemptions (search summary) — [same Act](https://old.fsvps.gov.ru/fsvps-docs/ru/importExport/iran/files/iran_law_import_export.pdf)
- EAEU guidance on Iranian trade regulation (2020) describes the card as required for "all persons whether natural or legal engaging in foreign trade". ICCIMA, the Iran Chamber of Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture, issues it — [EAEU Guidance on Iran Trade Regulation 2020](https://eec.eaeunion.org/upload/medialibrary/649/Guidance-on-the-Islamic-Republic-of-Iran-Trade-Regulation-2020.pdf)
- HISTORICAL (2020): rules capped exports by new card holders at USD 500,000 in their first year. The cap targeted "rented" cards (karte-e ejare'i) and aimed to improve repatriation of export earnings. Manufacturers who export were exempt — [Eghtesad Online, Aug 2020 (search summary)](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/750698/govt-tightens-export-rules); [Eghtesad Online, Sep 2020: "Strictures on use of commercial cards"](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/751082/strictures-on-use-of-commercial-cards)
- HISTORICAL (2021): of 24,115 exporters, 11,486 held the mandatory commercial cards — [Eghtesad Online, May 2021 (search summary)](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/786424/a-glance-at-export-income-repatriation)
- A Persian exporter guide lists the steps to export saffron:
  1. Price review at the pricing commission (kommision-e nerkh-gozari).
  2. Market selection.
  3. Sourcing and packaging.
  4. Permits: a standard certificate (govahi-e estandard), a health/phytosanitary certificate depending on product, and a commercial card.
  5. Customs export declaration (ezharnameh), filed by a representative in person.
  6. Destination-country documents (import permit, phytosanitary certificate).
  7. Physical inspection, after which customs issues the export licence (parvaneh-ye saderat).

  The page has no date — [Rahkar Tejarat Paya (Persian)](https://rahkartejarat.com/exporting/export-saffron/)
- The same guide says Iranian-passport travellers may carry up to 100 g of saffron. It also says carrying saffron to Saudi Arabia, Syria or Iraq is prohibited. It gives no limit for foreign-passport holders — [Rahkar Tejarat (Persian)](https://rahkartejarat.com/exporting/export-saffron/)

**Saffron-specific rules: bulk vs packaged, duties, price floors**
- HISTORICAL (Oct 2013): Iran eliminated its saffron export duty. Until then, a 5% export duty applied to saffron exported in packs over 30 g — [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/intervention/17744-iran-elimination-of-export-duty-on-saffron)
- A freight-forwarder blog still cites a 5% tariff on base price for bulk saffron in packs over 30 g, under a 2008 resolution. This conflicts with the 2013 elimination recorded by Global Trade Alert. The current status is unverified — [Persian Forwarder (Persian)](https://persianforwarder.com/blog/saffron-tariff/)
- Claimed bulk-export ban, HISTORICAL (c. 2021, unconfirmed): exporter Ali Shariati Moghadam said a Supreme Leader directive bans exports of saffron and dates in raw or bulk form. No official decree was found — [Radio Tehran (Persian)](http://radiotehran.ir/NewsDetails/?m=108102&n=728606)
- The same guide says the government restricted bulk (faleh) exports in recent years, citing re-export of Iranian saffron by Spain and the UAE. Bulk trade uses 0.5 kg and 1 kg units. Packaged trade is in grams or mesghal — [Rahkar Tejarat (Persian)](https://rahkartejarat.com/exporting/export-saffron/)
- HISTORICAL (1402 / 2023-24): exports in small packs under 30 g grew to 22% of exported saffron. Exporters say most saffron leaves Iran in bulk and foreign buyers repackage it under their own brands — [Eghtesad Online, May 2024 (search summary)](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/841642/iran-exports-207-m-worth-of-saffron-to-55-countries)
- A Tabnak headline (undated) says only 7% of Iranian saffron is exported packaged. The same headline says Afghan, Pakistani and Chinese traders smuggle saffron out of Iran — [Tabnak (Persian, headline only; page returned 503)](https://www.tabnak.ir/fa/news/1011537/)
- HISTORICAL (2019): exporters complained that government-prescribed export prices and currency rules pushed saffron smuggling toward Afghanistan. There it is repackaged and re-exported, for example to India — [YJC (search summary)](https://www.yjc.ir/en/news/43283/iran-saffron-exports-down-due-to-smuggling-government-restrictions-exporter)
- An official once said the official export price was set above what exporters received, causing a loss of about USD 300/kg under the exchange-rate system. The year is unclear — [Trend.az (search summary)](https://www.trend.az/business/economy/3628511.html)
- The Mashhad Chamber of Commerce has called for removing the currency-repatriation obligation for saffron as an export incentive. The page is undated — [MCCIMA news (Persian)](https://news.mccima.com/1130/)

**Standards (INSO / ISO 3632) and labs**
- Iran's national saffron standard is INSO 259 (259-2 covers test methods). Iranian studies measure crocin, picrocrocin and safranal against both INSO 259-2 and ISO 3632 — [University of Birjand conference paper (search summary)](https://cv.birjand.ac.ir/hamami/en/articlesInConferences/50279)
- An FAO tender (TCP/IRA/3902, issued 8 June 2025) requires saffron samples to be analysed under "Iran National Standard No. 3632". It names Razavi Khorasan, North Khorasan and South Khorasan as "the three primary saffron-producing provinces". It plans training for staff of "at least 15 IFDA-accredited laboratories" in Tehran and the three Khorasan provinces. IFDA is the Iran Food and Drug Administration. It also plans GMP (drying and storage) training for farmers and traders. Deliverables were due 15 Oct and 15 Dec 2025 — [FAO Iran tender PDF via UN Iran](https://iran.un.org/en/download/185645/295888)
- In September 2025, FAO signed an agreement with Mashhad University of Medical Sciences to improve authenticity testing of Iranian saffron — [Eghtesad Online, 16 Sep 2025 (search summary)](https://www.eghtesadonline.com/en/news/2001885/fao-mashhad-university-ink-agreement-on-iran-saffron-market)
- HISTORICAL (2020): an exporter quoted by IRNA said European countries do not recognise Iranian saffron standards. Iranian officials proposed an exporters' bloc to set saffron standards, objecting to European-led benchmarking — [Iran Times, Jan 2020 (search summary)](https://iran-times.com/?p=17752)
- A commercial import guide claims every shipment is tested in labs approved by ISIRI, the former name of INSO. This was not confirmed as a legal requirement — [commercial guide (low quality)](https://new-staffschool.unilag.edu.ng/iranwars28/how-to-import-saffron-from-iran.html)

**Currency repatriation (rafe' ta'ahod-e arzi), 2026**
- September 2026 reports, based on an FT report, say the Central Bank of Iran eased foreign-exchange controls. Exporters may reportedly repatriate earnings via USDT or Bitcoin on domestic exchanges and use the proceeds to finance imports, instead of selling currency on the government platform below market rates. No primary CBI circular was found. One outlet calls the change unofficial — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/iran-central-bank-encourages-crypto-evade-sanctions); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/iran-encourages-traders-to-repatriate-overseas-earnings-through-crypto/); [crypto.news citing FT](https://crypto.news/iran-turns-to-crypto-for-export-payments/)
- The deadline to clear 1404 (2025-26) export-currency obligations was extended to the end of Azar 1405 (about 21 Dec 2026). The implementing infrastructure was reportedly not yet ready — [Arman-e Kasb-o-Kar (Persian, search summary)](https://armanekasbokar.ir/%D9%85%D9%87%D9%84%D8%AA-%D8%B1%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%87%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B2%DB%8C-%D8%B3%D8%A7%D9%84-%DB%B1%DB%B4%DB%B0%DB%B4-%D8%AA%D8%A7-%D9%BE%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A2%D8%B0%D8%B1/)
- TPO (Trade Promotion Organization) says expired obligations from 1401 onward can be cleared by importing basic goods. The resolution was sent to banks on 10 Khordad 1405 (31 May 2026) — [Rahbord-e Mo'aser (Persian, search summary)](https://rahbordemoaser.ir/fa/news/309662/)
- Unfulfilled obligations from 1397–1400 (2018–2022) can be settled in rials until the end of 1405 (March 2027), by paying 10% (trading firms) or 5% (producers) of the euro-remittance buying rate — [Bank Avval (Persian, search summary)](https://bankavl.com/%D8%A8%D8%AE%D8%B4-%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-2/63169-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%86%DA%A9-%D9%85%D8%B1%DA%A9%D8%B2%DB%8C-%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%DA%A9%D8%B1%D8%AF-%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%B5%D8%AA-%D8%AC%D8%AF%DB%8C%D8%AF-%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C-%D8%B1%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%87%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B2%DB%8C-%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B1%DA%A9%D9%86%D9%86%D8%AF%DA%AF%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%A7-%D9%BE%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B3%D8%A7%D9%84)
- HISTORICAL (c. 2025): a CBI official told Mehr that exporters of petrochemicals, refining, steel, basic metals and petroleum products must offer 100% of export currency in CBI systems. Other commodity groups may use methods such as import-in-exchange-for-export — [search summary of Mehr-sourced report via madeinirani.com](https://madeinirani.com/export-import-news/iran-trade-news/implementation-of-import-in-exchange-for-export-for-5-major-export-groups/)

**Barberry export rules**
- The South Khorasan Agriculture Jihad organisation (Jihad-e Keshavarzi) issues barberry export permits. It issued permits for 140,782 kg in the first 6 months of 1405, against 349,166 kg in all of last year. Destinations: Iraq, UAE, Russia, Turkey, Germany, Australia. These permit statistics do not mean the goods clear customs in the province: exports leave through other provinces' customs. Report dated 19 Mehr 1405 (11 Oct 2026) — [IRIB News (Persian, search summary)](https://www.irib-news.ir/fa/news/5935397/); [SNN (Persian)](https://snn.ir/fa/news/1468642/)
- The South Khorasan governor said the Chamber of Commerce and Jihad-e Keshavarzi would hold a meeting to set a fair barberry price, so buyers purchase directly from farmers. He said the government would intervene on pricing. The article is undated — [Quds Online (Persian, search summary)](https://www.qudsonline.ir/news/1169141/)
- No barberry-specific export ban, quota or minimum price was found.

### Inferences
- A small Birjand farmer cannot lawfully export commercial quantities in his own name without a commercial card. The practical routes:
  - Sell to a card-holding exporter, packer or trading company that handles customs and the currency obligation.
  - Use an exporter's card (the "rented card" practice), which the authorities have tried to restrict.
  - Form or join a company or cooperative that obtains a card.
- The exporter of record, not the farmer, carries the currency-repatriation obligation. This is a big burden while foreign banking is cut off. It is a main reason why informal channels (passenger carriage, smuggling via Afghanistan) and relatives abroad are common.
- The pricing commission and the "standard certificate" step mean saffron moves through formal grading and pricing gates that raw barberries may not. Barberries appear to need mainly a Jihad-e Keshavarzi export permit and phytosanitary clearance.

### Gaps
- No primary 2026 text was found for: the current saffron export base price (qeymat-e payeh-ye saderati); any 2026 bulk-export ban or packaging mandate; or whether exporters must register with the Iran National Saffron Council. Iranian government sites (tpo.ir, irica.ir, iccima.ir) were not reachable or not surfaced.
- Whether the commercial-card exemption for frontier-zone cooperatives could cover any South Khorasan border-area cooperative (Afghan border) was not confirmed.
- Whether INSO certification is legally mandatory for every saffron export consignment in 2026 is unconfirmed. Whether an INSO barberry standard exists is also unconfirmed.

## 2. Documents and tests needed (Iran side, EU, Turkey)

### Takeaway
On the Iranian side, an exporter needs:
- a commercial card
- a customs declaration and export licence
- a standard (INSO/ISO 3632) certificate for saffron
- a health certificate (IFDA) and/or phytosanitary certificate
- a certificate of origin
- for barberries, a Jihad-e Keshavarzi export permit

For the EU, the essential evidence is lab results showing compliance with EU contaminant and pesticide limits and with saffron identity and grade standards. ISO 3632-1:2011 is the commercial benchmark. Iranian pistachios face special EU controls, but this research found no Iran-specific EU control regime for saffron or barberries.

### Cited Findings
- Iranian export documents named by a Persian exporter guide: standard certificate, health/phytosanitary certificate, commercial card, customs declaration, export licence issued after inspection. Destination-country documents, such as import permits and phytosanitary certificates, must be obtained before shipping — [Rahkar Tejarat (Persian)](https://rahkartejarat.com/exporting/export-saffron/)
- Barberry export permits are issued by the provincial Jihad-e Keshavarzi — [IRIB News, 11 Oct 2026 (search summary)](https://www.irib-news.ir/fa/news/5935397/)
- ISO 3632-1:2011 is "the most commonly requested standard for saffron and saffron powder worldwide" and sets three grades. ISO 3632-2:2010 sets the test methods. Codex general standards cover contaminants, additives and labelling — [ITC/Intracen "Red Gold Rush"](https://intracen.org/es/media/2507)
- Under ISO 3632, Category I saffron must have crocin (colouring strength) above 190 units, measured by UV-Vis spectrophotometry. HPLC testing for crocin, safranal and picrocrocin helps tell real saffron from safflower or turmeric substitutes — [YesWeLab (lab blog)](https://blog.yeswelab.fr/en/agrifood/laboratory-analysis-of-saffron/); [Nutrada (supplier)](https://nutrada.com/products/herbs/saffron-wholesale)
- ISO 3632 defines quality categories but "does not describe analytical techniques and methods for the detection of adulterants" — [University of Padua thesis](https://thesis.unipd.it/handle/20.500.12608/67821). An EU CORDIS project found an "important gap" in ISO 3632 for detecting colour additives — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/30195)
- EU special import controls under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1793:
  - Iranian pistachios have been under special conditions since 2014 for aflatoxins. They are listed in Annex II, with 50% identity and physical checks, plus an official certificate and sampling/analysis results for each consignment — [Iranian Chamber export guide on pistachio exports to the EU (Persian PDF)](https://www.eccim.com/uploads/user/2216/files/1401/11/02%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D9%BE%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%20%D8%A8%D9%87%20%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%DB%8C%D9%87%20%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%BE%D8%A7/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D9%BE%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%20%D8%A8%D9%87%20%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%DB%8C%D9%87%20%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%BE%D8%A7%201.pdf)
  - The Regulation was last amended by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1441 of 18 July 2025 (OJ 23 July 2025). The Commission must review the annexes at intervals of no more than 6 months — [EUR-Lex 2025/1441](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1441/oj/eng)
  - No Iranian saffron or barberry entry was found in the annexes. The only saffron item found was a UK (Scottish) proposal for 10% checks on Indian saffron for pesticide residues — [FSS consultation annex](https://consult.foodstandards.gov.scot/regulatory-policy/review-of-retained-regulation-2019-1793-on-increas/supporting_documents/Annex%20B%20%20Proposed%20amendments%20to%20Regulation%2020191793%20002.pdf)
- Food imported from third countries into the EU must comply with EU pesticide MRLs. RASFF notifications are issued when residues exceed a specific or default limit — [ScienceDirect study on RASFF pesticide notifications](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0956713521007192); [ResearchGate RASFF pesticide paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361958521_Notifications_on_Pesticide_Residues_in_the_Rapid_Alert_System_for_Food_and_Feed_RASFF)

### Inferences
- Minimum documents for a lawful commercial saffron shipment to an EU buyer:
  - Iranian export licence and customs declaration
  - commercial invoice and packing list
  - certificate of origin (Iranian Chamber of Commerce)
  - ISO 3632 lab report (crocin, picrocrocin, safranal, moisture, floral waste)
  - an adulteration screen (synthetic dyes, safflower, styles)
  - a pesticide multi-residue report

  For barberries, add aflatoxin and ochratoxin A results, a sulphite test and a pesticide screen. An EU-accredited lab report usually counts for more with EU buyers than an Iranian certificate, since Iranian standards have historically not been recognised in Europe.
- A phytosanitary certificate is probably not required by the EU for dried spices or dried fruit, as plant-health rules mainly target fresh plant material. Iranian procedures still issue one routinely. This inference is unverified: the other EU-side researchers should confirm it.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for the Iranian Chamber certificate-of-origin process or IFDA health-certificate fees and timelines in 2026.
- Turkish import or transit requirements for Iranian saffron and barberries were not researched in depth here: Turkish customs documents, TIR transit, Turkish Food Codex limits, and duty. Other researchers cover this.
- The consolidated 2019/1793 annex text as of October 2026 was not fetched, so absence of saffron or barberry entries is not fully confirmed.

## 3. Quality problems causing EU rejections; RASFF record

### Takeaway
Saffron adulteration is the dominant quality risk. Adulterants include synthetic dyes, safflower and added styles or other plant parts, and powder is most at risk. For Iranian dried fruit and nuts, the EU's main historical concern is aflatoxins: Iran is the most-notified origin for aflatoxins in RASFF overall, driven by pistachios. Search found no RASFF notifications specifically for Iranian saffron or barberries, but direct portal checks were not possible. Iranian barberries do carry documented mycotoxin risk (aflatoxins, OTA), and supplier claims point to pesticide risk (chlorpyrifos).

### Cited Findings
- FAO (June 2025): "Among the most commonly adulterated food products, saffron stands out due to its high market value and limited production." Economically motivated adulteration is "often difficult to detect with routine analytical methods" — [FAO Iran tender](https://iran.un.org/en/download/185645/295888)
- Adulteration "happens more often with the powdered saffron as less expensive spices can be added to increase the mass" — [ISO news, 2014](https://www.iso.org/news/2014/02/Ref1819.html)
- Safflower stigmas closely resemble saffron and are a common adulterant. A May 2025 study covers detecting them — [J. Analytical Methods in Chemistry 2025 (search summary)](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1155/jamc/6366923)
- A study of Tehran restaurants found synthetic colours in 52% of 573 saffron-solution and saffron-dish samples. This is domestic food, not exports, but it shows the prevalence of dye use — [PubMed 25116149](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25116149/)
- Mislabelling: Spanish police in Alicante arrested five people for selling "Spanish saffron" that came from Syria and Iran. Spain is described as a long-standing re-export hub for Iranian saffron — [Medium (secondary)](https://medium.com/@chaughney/smuggling-saffron-the-lesser-known-iranian-sanction-b1cb74de6cf)
- HISTORICAL (2017): a report alleged that genuine Iranian saffron is adulterated abroad, naming the UAE and Spain, and resold to high-end markets — [Financial Express (Bangladesh)](https://mail.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/adulteration-abroad-undercuts-iranian-saffrons-popularity-1505847248)
- RASFF aflatoxin notifications 1997–2022: Iran was the most frequently notified country (21.56%) — [ResearchGate RASFF mycotoxin analysis (search summary)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395394846_P01-07_Analysis_of_RASFF_Notifications_on_Mycotoxins_Evaluation_of_Aflatoxin_Incidence_and_Border_Rejection_Trends_in_Food_Products)
- HISTORICAL (2020): an INC border-rejection compilation lists ochratoxin A and sulphites as the most common reasons for dried-fruit notifications after aflatoxins. Iran had only one dried-fruit notification that year — [INC 2020 Border Rejections](https://inc.nutfruit.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2020_EU-USA-JAP-AUS_Border_Rejections_INC-1.pdf)
- An Iranian survey of barberries found 30% of samples contaminated with aflatoxins and 26.7% with ochratoxin A — [ResearchGate: Fungal contamination of barberry in Iran](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273619652_A_Survey_of_Fungal_Contamination_Associated_with_Barberry_Berberis_spp_in_Iran)
- An Iranian barberry supplier claims "Chlorpyrifos toxin is abundant in Iranian barberry products". It markets pesticide-free lots tested outside Iran. This is a marketing claim, not verified — [Artin Nuts](https://artinnuts.net/barberries-without-pesticides-iranian-organic-red-barberry/)
- A 2026 RASFF weekly digest shows sulphite border rejections do occur for berry products (juniper berries, North Macedonia). No Iranian barberry entry was found — [RASFF week 10 2026 digest](https://rasff.fruit-risk.com/week/2026-W10.html)
- An Agrolab RASFF summary for Aug–Sep 2025 had no saffron entry — [Agrolab RASFF radar 10/25](https://agrolab.com/de/aktuelles/lebensmittel-news/5881-warnmeldungen-rasff-lebensmittel-radar-10-25-de.html)

### Inferences
- The highest EU border or market-surveillance risk for a small-farmer saffron lot is failing authenticity or ISO grade, for example through style ("kanj") content, moisture, or added colour. Pre-shipment ISO 3632 plus adulteration testing is the main defence.
- For barberries, the risks are mycotoxins (aflatoxins, OTA) from poor drying and storage, pesticide residues (chlorpyrifos is no longer approved in the EU, so any detection is likely to fail), and possibly sulphite or colour treatments. Shade-dried, well-stored lots with a lab report are the defence.

### Gaps

## 4. Logistics: routes, air freight, courier/post, costs, transit times, insurance

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, logistics are severely disrupted:
- **Sea:** a US naval blockade covers all Iranian ports (since 14 July 2026).
- **Air:** almost all Iranian airlines were designated by the US Treasury on 8 Sept 2026, and most of their flights to Turkey, the UAE and China were cancelled. EASA has an active conflict-zone advisory for Iranian airspace.
- **Couriers:** major couriers (DHL at least) have suspended Iran service.

The main formal export route is overland by truck to Turkey via Bazargan–Gürbulak. There, freight rates rose about fivefold in two months (about USD 1,500 to USD 8,000 per truck, Turkey to Iran direction). Iranian customs delays run 12–25 days, and about 8,000 trucks were queued in October 2026. Pre-crisis benchmarks (USD ~2,500 per truck Istanbul–Tehran, 5–10 days) are now HISTORICAL.

### Cited Findings
**Road via Turkey**
- 11 Oct 2026: freight from Turkey to Iran rose from about USD 1,500 to USD 7,900–8,000 in under two months, according to Ehsan Malekzadeh, chairman of Iran's Association of International Transport Companies. About 8,000 trucks were stranded at Bazargan. Each stranded truck costs about USD 175 per day. Transport, insurance and logistics, previously no more than about 10% of a shipment's value, now substantially exceed that. The cause is the US naval blockade pushing trade onto land routes, plus US pressure on Turkey and Pakistan to cut Iran's overland routes. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called the campaign "absolute isolation" — [Iran International, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610114328)
- 28 Sep 2026: queues at Gürbulak–Bazargan stretched about 7 km, and Iranian-side customs delays ran 12–25 days. Truck arrivals rose from about 200 to about 1,200 (per an Iranian trade official). Turkey added lanes and scanning capacity at Gürbulak. Before the conflict, more than 80% of Iran's two-way trade by volume moved through southern maritime routes. The sources are unnamed — [INVC / International News and Views, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.internationalnewsandviews.com/iran-trade-crisis-turkiye-border-truck-queues-us-blockade-2026-409762-2/)
- About one-third of Iran's non-oil exports normally leave by land or air — [Wikipedia: 2026 US naval blockade of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_naval_blockade_of_Iran)
- HISTORICAL (pre-crisis): Istanbul–Tehran full truck about USD 2,500, 5–6 days, from a Turkish carrier page about 7 months old. Another estimate gave 5–10 working days — [Sayginlar Nakliyat](https://www.sayginlarnakliyat.com/en/iran-shipping/) (search summary); [Mobin Asa](https://mobinasa.com/en/international-destinations/shipping-from-turkey-to-iran/)
- A July 2026 border-crossing update described Bazargan–Gürbulak as "mostly stable" but busy. This was before the September–October congestion — [Border Crossing Hub (search summary)](https://bordercrossinghub.com/bazargan-gurbulak-border-crossing/)

**Air**
- The US Treasury designated 27 Iranian airlines on 8 Sep 2026, covering the remaining carriers not previously sanctioned. After a short wind-down period expired, all but a handful of Iranian flights to Turkey, the UAE and China were cancelled. Travellers are moving overland — [Al Jazeera, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/us-aviation-sanctions-disrupt-iran-flights-push-travellers-overland)
- EASA Conflict Zone Information Bulletin CZIB-2026-04-R2 (Airspace of Iran) is active, revised 30 Sep 2026 and valid until 16 Nov 2026 — [EASA CZIBs](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/air-operations/czibs) (search summary)
- Since 28 Feb 2026, Lufthansa Group, Air France-KLM, British Airways, Emirates, flydubai, Turkish Airlines and others suspended Iran service. The tracker's latest status is mid/late June 2026, so some flights may have resumed — [iranwarlive airspace tracker (search summary)](https://iranwarlive.com/airspace)
- Iran has threatened regional airports — [Al Jazeera, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/24/why-is-iran-threatening-regional-airports-and-what-could-be-the-impact)

**Courier and post**
- A WES notice dated 2 Mar 2026 lists DHL among carriers with confirmed suspensions to Iran. The same notice says mail services to and from the region were temporarily suspended in July 2025 — [WES notices](https://www.wes.org/important-wes-notices/)
- HISTORICAL (2019): FedEx made a voluntary self-disclosure to OFAC after packages were shipped into Iran via a UK subsidiary, with some delivered by Iran Post, and stopped the practice — [Willkie Compliance Concourse](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/news-alerts-2019-03-march-20190319-us-delivery-services-company-reports-iran/)

**Sanctions as logistics friction (not legal analysis)**
- A saffron retailer's blog says saffron itself was not named in the September and October 2026 US measures. It adds that aviation, rail and banking measures still make shipments harder. This is a commercial blog — [Saffronice (search summary)](https://saffronice.com/blogs/saffron-value-heritage/iran-sanctions-saffron-supply-routes)
- CENTCOM and Treasury froze about USD 350 million in Iranian crypto assets — [Wikipedia: blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_naval_blockade_of_Iran)

### Inferences
- **Route.** In October 2026 the realistic formal route from Birjand is by road:
  1. Birjand to Mashhad, about 470 km. This distance is general knowledge, not sourced.
  2. Mashhad to Tabriz and Bazargan.
  3. Bazargan–Gürbulak into Turkey.
  4. Onward by TIR or Turkish carrier to the EU, or delivery to relatives in Turkey.
- **Consolidation.** Saffron is high-value and low-weight, so a few kg would travel as groupage or LTL with a consolidator, not a full truck. Even so, crossing delays of 2–4 weeks and sharply higher costs apply.
- **Air.** Air freight out of Iran to Europe is effectively unavailable. Air cargo would need an Iranian carrier, which is US-designated, or a non-Iranian carrier operating into Iran (largely suspended). This inference rests on the airline designations and EASA advisory above.
- **Courier and passenger carriage.** International courier service from Iran (DHL/FedEx/UPS) appears unavailable. Passenger hand-carry (100 g limit for Iranian passport holders) over land is the informal small-quantity channel. Commercial quantities need a formal exporter.
- **Insurance.** Cargo insurance for Iran-origin goods in a war or blockade setting is likely expensive or unavailable from Western insurers. No specific source was found.

### Gaps
- Kapıköy–Razi crossing status and costs for October 2026 were not found. Neither were current groupage or LTL rates per kg Mashhad–Istanbul, or onward Turkey–EU road rates.
- No 2026 source was found on Iran Post EMS service to Turkey or the EU.
- No cargo-insurance pricing for Iran-origin food shipments was found.
- Whether Turkish Airlines or other Turkish carriers fly Tehran or Mashhad–Istanbul in October 2026 was not confirmed.

## 5. Market context: production shares, prices, buyers, EU share

### Takeaway
Iran is the dominant saffron producer and exporter. About 93% of Iranian saffron comes from the three Khorasan provinces. South Khorasan produced 25.35 t of dried saffron in 1404 (2025-26), a drought-reduced crop, and forecasts 48.3 t for 1405. South Khorasan holds a near-monopoly on barberries: over 95% of world production. Iranian exports in H1 1404 were 99.5 t worth USD 94 million, an implied unit value of about USD 945/kg. Top buyers were the UAE, Spain and China. Spain (EU) took about 20% of that half-year's volume, much of it re-exported or repacked. Domestic wholesale saffron prices in Mehr 1405 (October 2026) are about 298–315 million toman/kg, roughly USD 1,120–1,190/kg at the open-market rate.

- **Barberry value.** Barberries are low-value per kg compared with saffron. Freight costs at October 2026 truck rates make small barberry shipments uneconomic unless consolidated.

## 6. Small-scale realities: direct export vs licensed exporter or cooperative; fair-trade/co-op models

### Takeaway
A single small farmer cannot practically export directly. That would require a commercial card, a customs agent, a standard certificate, an IFDA health certificate, a Jihad permit for barberries and a currency-repatriation commitment, all under war and blockade conditions. The realistic models:
- Sell to a licensed exporter or packer in Birjand, Qaenat or Mashhad.
- Use a family-linked company that holds a card.
- Join a cooperative.

This research found no documented fair-trade or certified-cooperative export programme for South Khorasan saffron or barberries.

### Cited Findings
- A commercial card is required for commercial exports. The card-holder (or a representative with power of attorney) must file the customs declaration in person — [Export-Import Regulations Act](https://old.fsvps.gov.ru/fsvps-docs/ru/importExport/iran/files/iran_law_import_export.pdf); [Rahkar Tejarat](https://rahkartejarat.com/exporting/export-saffron/)
- A South Khorasan study surveyed 120 experts and exporters. It found that weak distribution channels, improper packaging and lack of publicity abroad hold back saffron and barberry exports — [Journal of Saffron Research, Univ. of Birjand](https://jsr.birjand.ac.ir/article_1170.html?lang=en) (search summary)
- A study of Qaenat villages (40 experts) ranked the economic factor first (weight 0.71), ahead of political and technical factors, in saffron production constraints — [Economic Geography Research, Zanjan Univ.](https://jurs.znu.ac.ir/article_717732_en.html) (search summary)
- The South Khorasan governor has pushed for direct purchase of barberries from farmers at a price set with the Chamber and Jihad-e Keshavarzi — [Quds Online](https://www.qudsonline.ir/news/1169141/) (search summary)
- The FAO project (2025) includes GMP training for saffron farmers and traders in the three Khorasan provinces. This is a channel for small-farmer quality upgrading — [FAO tender](https://iran.un.org/en/download/185645/295888)
- A Mehr headline, "Saffron on the exchange, farmer at a dead end", refers to Iran Mercantile Exchange saffron trading not benefiting farmers — [Mehr News (Persian, headline only; page returned 503)](https://www.mehrnews.com/news/6630395/)
- Research exists comparing saffron yield and quality of rural production-cooperative members against non-members in Iran. The findings were not retrieved — [ISHS](https://ishs.org/ishs-article/850_54/)

### Inferences
- The lowest-friction lawful path for the family:
  1. The farmer sells to (or tolls through) a South Khorasan or Mashhad exporter with a commercial card and an export track record to Turkey.
  2. The exporter ships packaged, ISO-3632-tested saffron and tested barberries by road to a buyer of record in Turkey or the EU.
  3. The couple's EU business buys from that exporter.

  Whether an EU business may buy Iranian-origin saffron is a sanctions question that other researchers cover.
- Hand-carried amounts (100 g limit for Iranian passport holders) cannot support a commercial business.

### Gaps
- No named South Khorasan saffron or barberry cooperatives with export licences were found, nor any women's cooperative in Qaenat or Birjand.
- No fair-trade certified (FLO/Fairtrade or Rainforest) Iranian saffron or barberry producer groups were found. No EU-organic-certified South Khorasan operators were found.
- The current fee or practice for using an exporter's card ("rented card") in 2026 was not found.
