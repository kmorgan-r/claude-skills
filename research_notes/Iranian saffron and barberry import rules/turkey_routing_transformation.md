# Routing Iranian saffron and dried barberries (zereshk) through Turkey, and processing them there, before sale in the EU and US (status: 18 October 2026)

Scope note: These notes cover only the Turkey routing and "transformation" question. EU food-safety rules, EU duty rates, Iranian export rules and payments are in sibling notes in this folder. Facts dated before 2026 are marked "historical" where the date matters.

---

## Q1. Does passing the goods through Turkey change anything legally (Turkish law, transit vs import/re-export, Turkish certificates of origin, Turkey's stance on sanctions)?

### Takeaway
Shipping through Turkey does not change the goods' Iranian origin under EU, US or Turkish origin rules. For the US it changes nothing: the statute bans Iranian-origin goods imported "directly or indirectly." Iran–Turkey trade in farm products is lawful under Turkish law, and Turkey gives Iran tariff concessions on farm goods. But a Turkish stopover only moves where the goods are, not where they come from.

### Cited Findings
- **US statute covers indirect routing.** CISADA, as codified, says: "no good or service of Iranian origin may be imported directly or indirectly into the United States." — [22 U.S.C. 8512(b)(1)(A), Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/8512)
- **US regulation.** "the importation into the United States of any goods or services of Iranian origin or owned or controlled by the Government of Iran, other than information and informational materials … is prohibited." — [31 CFR 560.201, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.201)
- **OFAC overview.** Iranian-origin goods "may not be imported into the United States, either directly or through third countries." This is an older OFAC brochure, so treat it as a historical summary, though the rule still stands. — [OFAC, "An overview of O.F.A.C. Regulations involving Sanctions against Iran"](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/iran.pdf)
- **Definition of "Iranian-origin goods."** It covers goods grown, produced, manufactured, extracted or processed in Iran, and goods that have entered Iranian commerce. Goods that only transited Iranian waters or ports, without otherwise contacting Iran, are excluded. Saffron and barberries grown in Birjand are therefore "grown … in Iran." — [31 CFR 560.306, Cornell LII (summary)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.306)
- **EU wholly-obtained rule.** The EU treats "vegetable products harvested there" as wholly obtained in that country. — [Delegated Reg. (EU) 2015/2446, Art. 31(b), EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **EU minimal operations.** The following do not confer origin:
  - "operations to ensure the preservation of products in good condition during transport and storage (ventilation, spreading out, drying, removal of damaged parts and similar operations) or operations facilitating shipment or transport"
  - "changes of packing and the breaking-up and assembly of consignments … and all other simple packaging operations"
  - "affixing of marks, labels"
  - combinations of these operations

  — [Delegated Reg. (EU) 2015/2446, Art. 34(a)–(h), EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Iran–Turkey Preferential Trade Agreement.**
  - In force since January 2015.
  - Turkey gives Iran tariff concessions on 138 agricultural tariff lines at the 6- and 8-digit level.
  - Iran cuts duties on 125 Turkish industrial product groups by 30–40%.

  — [ADASO analysis of the Türkiye–Iran PTA (Turkish)](https://adaso.org.tr/Content/WebDosyalar/Yayinlar/DisTicaretRaporlari/T%C3%9CRK%C4%B0YE%20%20%C4%B0RAN%20TERC%C4%B0HL%C4%B0%20T%C4%B0CARET%20ANLA%C5%9EMASI-ANAL%C4%B0Z-.pdf); [T.C. Ticaret Bakanlığı, Tercihli Ticaret Anlaşmaları](https://ticaret.gov.tr/dis-iliskiler/tercihli-ticaret-anlasmalari)
- **Saffron in the PTA concession list: unverified.** A search snippet of Decision 2014/6916 showed a row "0910.20 · Safran · 30% · 50 · 50%". I could not see the column meanings or the official text, so this is unverified. — [Search snippet only; Resmî Gazete original not retrieved](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2016/05/20160524-4.htm)
- **Turkey already imports Iranian saffron.** Its saffron imports come from a small set of countries: "İran, Afganistan, İspanya ve Özbekistan." Turkey is a net importer of saffron and was ranked 37th among world importers. — [Batı Akdeniz Kalkınma Ajansı, Safran Tarımı ve Endüstrisi Fizibilite Raporu 2024](https://baka.gov.tr/assets/upload/dosyalar/safran.pdf)
- **Turkey and the UN snapback.**
  - Turkey applied the reinstated UN measures in October 2025. It froze assets of Iran's Atomic Energy Organization, Bank Sepah and nuclear-linked firms on 1 October 2025.
  - Turkish officials continue to push for more trade with Iran, and Foreign Minister Fidan called the sanctions "unfair."

  — [Export Compliance Daily, 7 Oct 2025](https://exportcompliancedaily.com/news/2025/10/07/Turkey-Announces-Snapback-Sanctions-Against-Iran-2510060013); [Iran International, 4 Oct 2025](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202510046525); [Algemeiner, 2 Dec 2025](https://www.algemeiner.com/2025/12/02/turkey-pushes-closer-ties-iran-despite-mounting-sanctions-both-countries-pursue-regional-ambitions/)
- **Turkey and unilateral sanctions (historical, 2010).** Turkey said it is bound by UN sanctions but "not obliged to follow non-UN sanctions." I found no current official restatement. — [VOA, 2010](https://www.voanews.com/a/wait-and-see-game-for-turkeys-enforcement-of-un-sanctions-on-iran-96816624/120095.html)
- **Who issues certificates of origin.** Turkish chambers (odalar) certify non-preferential certificates of origin (menşe şahadetnamesi), which give no tariff preference. For exports to Iran and Pakistan, chambers issue the ECO certificate of origin. — [Lires customs guide (Turkish)](https://www.lires.com.tr/post/g%C3%BCmr%C3%BCkte-men%C5%9Fe-kurallar%C4%B1-ve-ispat-belgeleri-rehberi-eur-1-a-tr-ve-men%C5%9Fe-%C5%9Fahadetnamesi); [muhasebetr.com](https://www.muhasebetr.com/yazarlarimiz/ufuk/007)

### Inferences
- **Transit vs import into Turkey.**
  - Goods moved under Turkish transit (TIR/NCTS) are never released for free circulation in Turkey. Nothing happens to them legally there.
  - Goods imported into Turkey pay Turkish duty, possibly at the PTA preferential rate, and must clear Turkish food import controls. They can then be re-exported.
  - In both cases the transport documents will show Iran as the country of dispatch or origin. EU and US customs and banks can see that trail.
- **No genuine "Turkish" certificate.** A Turkish chamber certifying non-preferential origin should apply Turkey's own wholly-obtained and last-substantial-processing rules. These mirror the EU rules because of the customs union, so Iranian saffron repacked in Turkey should get an Iran-origin certificate, not a Turkish one. Asking a chamber to certify it as "Türk menşeli" would mean a false statement to a Turkish public body.
- **Turkish law does not make the trade illegal.** Turkish sanctions practice follows the UN, and the UN snapback measures target nuclear, missile and arms matters and listed entities, not food. So no Turkish rule blocks importing Birjand saffron or barberries, provided the counterparties and banks are not UN-listed.
- **Turkey's tolerance does not protect anyone from US law.** US exposure follows US persons, the US dollar and the US market, not Turkish law.

### Gaps
- **Turkish import procedure.** I could not retrieve the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (Tarım ve Orman Bakanlığı) procedure for importing Iranian spices and dried fruit (kontrol belgesi or TAREKS steps). Turkish government sites (ticaret.gov.tr) failed to resolve or reset the connection during this session.
- **Turkish duty rates.** I could not confirm the current Turkish MFN or PTA duty for 0910.20 (saffron) or 0813.40 (barberries), or whether saffron is on Turkey's PTA concession list.
- **Re-export certificates.** No primary source found on Turkish chamber practice for certificates on re-exported foreign-origin goods. The applicable Turkish rules are Customs Law 4458 on origin and the certificate-of-origin regulation.
- **Turkey and the transit convention.** Not verified this session: that Turkey is a party to the Common Transit Convention (NCTS T1/T2, since 2012).

## Q2. EU: what level of processing in Turkey (or elsewhere) would change the non-preferential origin, and can Iranian goods re-exported from Turkey get EU–Turkey preferences?

### Takeaway
Whole saffron and dried barberries are "wholly obtained" in Iran. Drying, cleaning, sorting, packing and labelling in Turkey are expressly listed as non-origin-conferring "minimal operations."

Only a real manufacturing step can give Turkish non-preferential origin. Examples are sugar confectionery (heading-change rule) or blends where Turkish ingredients exceed 50% of the weight. Even then, the product cannot claim EU–Turkey agricultural preferences, because Iranian inputs are not "originating."

In any case, the EU does not ban Iranian saffron or barberries (see sibling EU note). So for the EU, a change of origin mostly matters for labelling and duties, not legality.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 34 list.** The full text of the minimal-operations article (drying, sifting, sorting, washing, cutting up, repacking, labelling, putting up in sets, and combinations) is quoted in Q1. Note: grinding or milling is not named in Art. 34. — [2015/2446 Art. 34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Art. 32.** "Goods listed in Annex 22-01 shall be considered to have undergone their last substantial processing or working … in the country or territory in which the rules set out in that Annex are fulfilled." — [2015/2446 Art. 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Art. 33 (not economically justified).** "Any processing or working operation carried out in another country or territory shall be deemed not to be economically justified if it is established on the basis of the available facts that the purpose of that operation was to avoid the application of the measures referred to in Article 59 of the Code."
  - For goods not covered by Annex 22-01, origin then falls back to "the country or territory where the major portion of the materials originated, as determined on the basis of the value of the materials."

  — [2015/2446 Art. 33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Annex 22-01, Chapter 9 (coffee, tea, maté and spices).** In the 2015 text, the only Chapter 9 primary rules are for coffee (0901 11/12/21/22). There is no specific rule for saffron (0910 20). The Chapter 9 residual rules read:
  - "(2) The origin of a mixture of products of this Chapter shall be the country of origin of the materials that account for more than 50 % by weight of the mixture. The weight of materials of the same origin shall be taken together. (3) When none of the materials used meet the percentage required, the origin of the mixture shall be the country in which the mixing was carried out."
  - "'mixing' means the deliberate and proportionally controlled operation consisting in bringing together two or more fungible materials."
  - Residual rule: "the country in which the major portion of the materials originated, as determined on the basis of the weight of the materials."

  — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01, Section II, Chapter 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Annex 22-01, Chapter 17.** "1704 Sugar confectionery (including white chocolate), not containing cocoa — CTH." CTH means a change to the heading from any other heading. — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01, Chapter 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **Annex 22-01, Chapter 20 (fruit preparations, jams, juices).**
  - Mixture residual rule: origin is the country of origin of materials over 50% by weight. For 2009 juices, it is over 50% by weight of dry matter. The 2021 HS-2022 amendment confirmed this wording.
  - General residual rule: major portion of materials by weight.
  - The only listed primary rule is ex 2009 grape juice.

  — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01, Chapter 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446); [Council doc ST-11379-2021-ADD-1 (2021 amendment, HS 2022)](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-11379-2021-ADD-1/en/pdf)
- **Chapters not in Annex 22-01.** Chapter 8 (fruit, including dried fruit 0813), Chapter 13 (extracts), Chapter 19 and Chapter 21 (2101 tea extracts, 2106 food preparations) have no rules in Annex 22-01. For these, origin follows the general UCC Art. 60 test (last substantial, economically justified processing producing a new product or an important stage of manufacture) together with Art. 34. — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01 chapter list (Chapters 2, 4, 9, 14, 17, 20, 22, 34, 35, 42, 49, 50–63 …)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **EU–Turkey trade regime for farm goods.**
  - The customs union does not cover basic agricultural products. They fall under a separate preferential regime (Decision 1/98) that needs a proof of preferential origin (EUR.1 / EUR-MED), not an A.TR.
  - An A.TR "is not a proof of origin – it is proof that the goods are in free circulation in the Customs Union."

  — [Irish Revenue A.TR guide](https://www.revenue.ie/ga/customs/documents/atr-guide.pdf); [Swedish Customs (Tullverket), A.TR](https://www.tullverket.se/en/startpage/business/internationalbusiness/exportinggoods/freetradewhenexporting/exportcertificates/movementcertificateatr/movementcertificateatrauthorisationtomakeout.4.422c8052175b1d827693d59.html); [KOTO (Konya chamber) on A.TR](https://koto.org.tr/en/atr-dolasim-belgesi-sd-5c5344029cfaa)
- **Processed farm products: sources conflict.** Some sources say processed agricultural goods are inside the customs union (A.TR). KOTO says items such as fruit juices, canned fruit and dried foods are "not included in the Customs Union" and need EUR.1. — [KOTO](https://koto.org.tr/en/atr-dolasim-belgesi-sd-5c5344029cfaa) contradicted by [celna24 glossary](https://www.celna24.pl/customs-glossary/a-tr-movement-certificate) and [The Trade Hub glossary](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/glossary/certificat-atr)
- **Rules of origin for Decision 1/98.**
  - Origin rules are in Protocol 3, which Association Council Decision 3/2006 replaced.
  - Council Decision (EU) 2024/3208 of 5 December 2024 set the EU position to replace Protocol 3 with a dynamic reference to the PEM Convention rules (Joint Committee Decision 1/2023).

  — [Council Decision (EU) 2024/3208, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2024/3208/oj/eng); [Council Decision (EU) 2020/2073](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudn/2020/2073/contents/data.html)
- **EU snapback sanctions do not touch food.**
  - The EU re-imposed Iran sanctions under Reg. 267/2012 from 29–30 September 2025, through Regs. 2025/1975, 2025/1980 and 2025/1982.
  - Trade restrictions cover dual-use items, sensitive technology, and energy, naval, industrial software, graphite and metals goods. Food or agricultural goods are not among them.

  — [Mayer Brown, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/corporate-governance-compliance/art/eu-iran-reimposing-sanctions)

### Inferences
- **Whole saffron (0910 20 10)** cleaned, graded, repacked or relabelled in Turkey stays of Iranian origin. It is wholly obtained (Art. 31(b)), and Turkish operations fall under Art. 34(a)–(e)/(h).
- **Dried barberries (0813 40)** sorted, washed, re-dried or packed in Turkey stay of Iranian origin, for the same reasons.
- **Ground saffron.** Grinding is not on the Art. 34 list. But Chapter 9 has no saffron rule, and grinding a single wholly-obtained spice arguably does not create "a new product." Under the general Art. 60(2) test, EU customs could well treat ground saffron as still Iranian. Its legal status is uncertain, so get a Binding Origin Information (BOI) decision first.
- **Saffron blends** (spice mixes, saffron-flavoured tea). Under the Chapter 9 mixture rule, origin follows the ingredient group over 50% by weight. Saffron is used in tiny weights, so a blend that is mostly Turkish tea or Turkish spices would usually be Turkish origin.
  - Caveat 1: Art. 32 speaks of "goods listed in Annex 22-01." It is unclear whether the Chapter 9 residual rules reach saffron mixtures when no primary rule lists them.
  - Caveat 2: Art. 33 (operation done to avoid measures) can override the result.
- **Sugar confectionery** with saffron (1704, e.g. saffron rock candy, nabat-style) made in Turkey from sugar of 1701 and saffron of 0910 meets the CTH rule. It would be Turkish non-preferential origin.
- **Barberry jam (2007) and juice (2009).** There is no listed primary rule. Under the general test, cooking fruit into jam is normally a new product. But the Chapter 20 residual rules point to the major portion of materials by weight. If barberries dominate the weight, there is a real risk the residual rule returns "Iran." Get a BOI.
- **No EU–Turkey preference.** None of these routes earns a preference. Iranian inputs are non-originating, Iran is outside the PEM zone, and PEM-type rules for Chapters 8–9 require wholly obtained fruit (inference from the standard PEM list-rule pattern; not verified this session). Turkish relatives would pay the EU MFN duty either way.
- **Origin still matters for EU labelling.** Origin determines what the label may say. Selling Iranian saffron as "Product of Turkey" could mislead consumers (see Q5). It does not determine whether the goods may enter the EU, because the EU has no ban on these goods.

### Gaps
- **Consolidated Annex 22-01.** I reviewed the 2015 original and the 2021 amendment. I did not confirm whether a later amendment (2022–2026) added rules for 0910 or 0813.
- **Commission "list rules."** I did not retrieve the non-binding rules the Commission publishes for products not in Annex 22-01, so I have no EU guidance specific to ground saffron or barberry jam.

## Q3. US: what processing changes origin (CBP "name, character, use"), how does marking apply, and does OFAC still treat a transformed product as Iranian?

### Takeaway
CBP looks for a new article with a new name, character or use. CBP has ruled that grinding and sterilising a spice, roasting nuts, and repacking do not change origin. Seasoning or cooking into a new product sometimes does.

The key sanctions rule is 31 CFR 560.407. Third-country products containing Iranian raw materials may enter the US only if those materials were "incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country *by a person other than a United States person*." Otherwise the goods remain prohibited.

Whole or ground saffron and dried barberries repacked in Turkey therefore remain banned Iranian-origin goods. A genuinely new manufactured product made by a non-US company can be lawful. US persons cannot take part in sourcing or processing the Iranian inputs.

### Cited Findings
- **The OFAC safe harbour.**
  - Paragraph (a): "Importation into the United States from third countries of goods containing Iranian-origin raw materials or components and transactions relating to such goods are not prohibited by § 560.201 or § 560.206 if those raw materials or components have been incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country by a person other than a United States person."
  - Paragraph (b): "Transactions relating to Iranian-origin goods that have not been incorporated into manufactured products or substantially transformed in a third country are prohibited."

  — [31 CFR 560.407, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.407)
- **US persons may not deal in Iranian goods anywhere.** Section 560.206 bars them from transactions in Iranian-origin goods: "no United States person, wherever located, may engage in any transaction or dealing" in goods of Iranian origin. — [31 CFR 560.206](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.206)
- **Facilitation.** US persons, wherever located, may not "approve, finance, facilitate, or guarantee" a foreign person's transaction that would be prohibited if a US person did it. — [31 CFR 560.208 (summary)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.208)
- **Who is a US person.** "any United States citizen, permanent resident alien, entity organized under the laws of the United States … or any person in the United States." — [31 CFR 560.314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.314)
- **CBP test.**
  - Substantial transformation needs "a new and different article [that] must emerge, 'having distinctive name, character, or use'" (Anheuser-Busch, 1908).
  - "Country of origin" is "the country of manufacture, production or growth" (19 CFR 134.1(b)).

  — [CBP NY N030426 (2008)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N030426)
- **Grinding a spice: no change (2008).** Indian black pepper steam-sterilised and ground in Vietnam was not substantially transformed, so origin stayed India. CBP cited C.S.D. 86-28 (HQ 729365), HQ 084928, HQ 734989 and HQ 560274. — [CBP NY N030426](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N030426)
- **Drying and grinding fresh peppers: change (2026).** Fresh Chinese chili peppers, frozen, then dried, heat-sterilised and ground in South Korea were "substantially transformed," so origin was Korea. This was a different starting article: fresh or frozen produce, not an already-dried spice. — [CBP NY N356879 (9 Jan 2026)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N356879)
- **Roasting nuts: no change.** Raw Iranian pistachios (and Chinese, Indian and US nuts) sorted, cleaned, dry-roasted and packed in Hong Kong were not substantially transformed, so origin stayed with the raw nut.
  - CBP cited "HQR 730058 … roasting of pecan nuts did not result in a substantial transformation" and "TD 85-158 … roasting of green pistachios did not constitute a substantial transformation."
  - By contrast, "Honey Roasted Peanuts" and "Mustard Peanuts" were substantially transformed.
  - Historical: this 2002 ruling dates from the 2000–2010 window when Iranian foodstuffs were licensable.

  — [CBP NY I83434 (2002)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/I83434)
- **Mixing and repacking.** "if the manufacturing or combining process is merely a minor one that leaves the identity of the article intact, a substantial transformation has not occurred." — [CBP NY N281444 (2017)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N281444)
- **Classification of Iranian barberries (historical).** Dried Iranian barberries (Berberis vulgaris) are classified in HTS 0813.40.1500 ("berries … barberries"). The duty then was 3.5¢/kg. — [CBP NY I87303 (2002)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/I87303)
- **Marking exemption for spices.**
  - 19 U.S.C. 1304(g) exempts spices of subheadings 0910.10–0910.99, including 0910.20 (saffron), from the marking duties of subsections (a) and (b).
  - Dried fruit (0813) is not exempt.
  - Unmarked goods that must be marked attract an extra 10% ad valorem duty (subsection (i)).
  - Removing or altering a required mark "with intent to conceal" is punishable by fines up to $100,000 for a first offence and $250,000 for later offences, and/or imprisonment (subsection (l)).

  — [19 U.S.C. 1304, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1304); [CBP NY N030426 on the 1996 coffee/tea/spice marking exemption](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N030426)
- **Legislative history of the safe harbour.** Commentary notes that a 2013 House bill would have studied repealing the substantial-transformation safe harbour for oil. It stalled. — [Search summary of Lexology commentary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d2006199-0462-49e1-806e-449ac621c9d5)
- **Status in 2026: no relief for food imports.**
  - GL U (March 2026) and GL X (21–22 June 2026, after a 17 June 2026 US–Iran MOU) authorised only Iranian crude, petroleum and petrochemicals.
  - GL X was replaced by GL X1 on 7 July 2026, with wind-down to 17 July 2026.
  - On 24 August 2026 ("Operation Economic Outcast"), OFAC extended EO 13902 secondary-sanctions authority to aviation, digital assets, gold, shipping and technology.
  - The same day it suspended five ITSR general licences: 560.544, 560.550 (non-commercial personal remittances), 560.554, GL F and GL G. Wind-down ran to 8 September 2026.
  - None of these sources mentions any relaxation for Iranian foodstuffs.

  — [Foley, July 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/07/ofac-rescinds-iranian-sanctions-relaxation-following-renewed-conflict/); [Greenberg Traurig, July 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/7/us-iran-sanctions-update-ofac-issues-general-license-x-following-signing-of-memorandum-of-understanding); [International Trade Insights, Aug 2026](https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/08/ofac-and-state-department-announces-new-updates-to-sdn-list-and-sanctions-related-to-iran/); [Expeditors newsflash, 25 Aug 2026](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/us-announces-new-sanctions-on-iran-suspends-general-licenses)
- **The 2010 revocation (historical).** Effective 29 September 2010, CISADA revoked the authorisation to import Iranian-origin foodstuffs and carpets. Under 22 U.S.C. 8512(d)(2), "No exception to the prohibition under subsection (b)(1) may be made for the commercial importation" of those goods unless the President issues a regulation with a certification and report to Congress. — [22 U.S.C. 8512, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/22/8512); [Federal Register list, 28 Sep 2010 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/agencies/department-of-treasury/foreign-assets-control-office/2010/09/28)

### Inferences
- **Whole saffron repacked in Turkey** is not substantially transformed (N030426, N281444 logic). It is Iranian under 560.306 and 560.407(b), so importing it into the US is prohibited. The 1304(g) marking exemption only means no retail label is required. Origin must still be declared truthfully on the entry, and the sanctions ban still applies.
- **Ground saffron.** N030426 is the closer analogy than N356879: saffron arrives already dried, as pepper did in N030426. So ground saffron would very likely remain Iranian.
- **Dried barberries re-dried, cleaned or packed** remain Iranian (by analogy to the roasting rulings TD 85-158 and I83434) and are prohibited.
- **Products more likely to qualify as "manufactured products"** under 560.407(a), when made by a non-US firm in Turkey (each needs a CBP ruling to be safe):
  - multi-ingredient confectionery
  - saffron-infused syrups or beverages
  - barberry jam with substantial added ingredients and cooking
  - seasoned or prepared mixes where saffron or barberry is a minor flavouring
- **OFAC's test is undefined.** OFAC has not published a separate definition of "substantially transformed" for 560.407. Practitioners generally use the CBP test. The evasion rule (560.203) is a backstop: OFAC can treat a sham or trivial process designed to launder origin as evasion.
- **The couple's own status is decisive.**
  - If the couple are US citizens, green-card holders or anyone in the US, they personally cannot buy Iranian saffron, finance or arrange its processing in Turkey, or broker it (560.206/560.208). This holds even if a Turkish company does the transforming.
  - They could only buy and resell the finished, genuinely transformed product at arm's length.
  - Separately, the suspension of 560.550 since 24 August 2026 removes the general licence US persons used for non-commercial personal remittances to family in Iran (see the sibling payments note).
- **Non-US Turkish relatives** selling a genuinely transformed product to the US are not violating 560.201 through the import itself. But they could "cause" violations under 560.203 if they use USD clearing for Iranian-origin inputs (see the Adani case in Q5).

### Gaps
- **No saffron-specific ruling.** I found no CBP ruling on the origin of Iranian saffron processed abroad, and no OFAC FAQ or ruling applying 560.407 to food products. OFAC's FAQ pages were not reachable through eCFR, which redirected to an "unblock" page.
- **No OFAC "substantial transformation" definition.** None found specific to 560.407.
- **2016–2018 JCPOA licence: not re-verified.** From background knowledge, not confirmed this session: OFAC re-authorised Iranian foodstuffs and carpets from January 2016 and revoked that authorisation with a wind-down ending 6 August 2018.
- **October 2026 OFAC actions.** Content not verified. Only a title, "Operation Economic Outcast Takes Unprecedented Action Against Sanctions Evasion Network Used by Iran," dated 1 October 2026, appeared in [IranWatch](https://www.iranwatch.org/library/government/i).

## Q4. Realistic "transformed product" options made in Turkey: legal status in the EU and US and practical burden

### Takeaway
Only products where saffron or barberry becomes one ingredient of a genuinely new manufactured food have a credible path to the US. Each such product needs a Turkish manufacturer (not a US person), a CBP origin ruling, and full US FDA food-facility compliance.

For the EU, transformation is legally unnecessary, since Iranian-origin saffron and barberries may be sold if correctly labelled. It mainly changes the origin statement and adds regulatory burden.

### Cited Findings
- **EU rule for sugar confectionery (1704):** CTH, so making it from sugar and saffron in Turkey confers Turkish origin. — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01 Ch. 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **EU mixture rule for Chapter 9** (tea and spice blends) is the 50%-by-weight rule. For Chapter 20 (jams, juices) it is 50% by weight, or by dry matter for juices. — [2015/2446 Annex 22-01 Ch. 9 & 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015R2446)
- **US flavouring precedent:** seasoning and roasting into "Honey Roasted"/"Mustard" peanuts was a substantial transformation; plain roasting was not. — [CBP NY I83434](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/I83434)
- **US blending precedents.** Blends of multi-origin spices with fillers, mixed in Taiwan, were classified as 2106.90 food preparations. The marking analysis then turned on whether the blending changed identity. — [CBP NY N260554 (2015)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N260554); [CBP NY N281444 (2017)](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N281444)
- **US sanctions condition:** transformation must be "by a person other than a United States person." — [31 CFR 560.407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.407)

### Inferences
Option-by-option assessment. "EU" means EU non-preferential origin and "US" means US sanctions and customs status. All are inferences from the rules above; none has a product-specific ruling.

| Product made in Turkey | EU origin (likely) | US status (likely) | Burden / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole saffron, repacked/relabelled | Iran (Art. 31(b), 34) | Prohibited (560.201, 560.407(b)) | — |
| Ground saffron | Probably Iran; uncertain | Prohibited (N030426 analogy) | — |
| Dried barberries, cleaned/packed | Iran | Prohibited | — |
| Saffron tea blend (Turkish tea >50% by weight, small saffron share) | Turkey under Ch. 9 mixture rule, subject to Art. 33 | Arguable "manufactured product." Blending may be "minor" if tea identity unchanged (N281444 logic); needs CBP ruling | Low processing burden |
| Saffron rock candy / lokum / confectionery | Turkey (CTH, 1704) | Strong candidate under 560.407(a) if made by a non-US Turkish firm | Food-facility registration and labelling |
| Barberry jam / juice / syrup | Uncertain (Ch. 20 residual weight rule could return Iran); get BOI | Plausible new product (cooking, added sugar); needs CBP ruling | Moderate; acidified-food rules may apply to some products in the US |
| Saffron extract / tincture | General Art. 60(2) test; extraction likely confers origin | Plausible manufactured product; OFAC/CBP ruling advisable | High burden: food-additive or colour status in the EU and US |
| Rice mix with saffron sachet (a "set") | Art. 34(d): sets do not confer origin | Saffron component remains Iranian, so prohibited | — |

- **Commercial point.** The US-legal products are new Turkish-branded foods with Iranian ingredients, not "Persian saffron." Labels may not imply the product is Iranian saffron sold as such, and may not hide ingredient origin where the label requires it.

### Gaps
- No product-specific CBP or OFAC rulings exist for saffron confectionery, saffron syrup or barberry jam made in third countries from Iranian inputs. A CBP binding ruling under 19 CFR Part 177, and possibly an OFAC interpretive request, would be needed.
- FDA, EU novel-food, additive and labelling details for extracts are not covered here.

## Q5. Transshipment to disguise origin: what counts as evasion or customs fraud (US, EU, Turkey), penalties, and enforcement examples

### Takeaway
Re-labelling Iranian saffron or barberries as Turkish, Afghan or Spanish is illegal in every relevant jurisdiction:
- **US:** sanctions evasion plus customs fraud and smuggling.
- **EU:** false customs declaration plus misleading food origin information.
- **Turkey:** false certificate of origin.

US penalties reach $1 million and 20 years per criminal ITSR violation. OFAC's May 2026 Adani settlement ($275 million) shows how forged origin trails are investigated, and that non-US firms can be liable for "causing" US-person violations.

### Cited Findings
- **US evasion rule.** "Any transaction … that evades or avoids, has the purpose of evading or avoiding, causes a violation of, or attempts to violate any of the prohibitions set forth in this part is prohibited." Conspiracies are also prohibited. — [31 CFR 560.203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.203)
- **ITSR penalties.**
  - Civil: "the greater of $377,700 or an amount that is twice the amount of the transaction that is the basis of the violation," inflation-adjusted.
  - Criminal (wilful): "fined not more than $1,000,000, or if a natural person, be imprisoned for not more than 20 years, or both."

  — [31 CFR 560.701](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.701)
- **Detention and forfeiture.** Iranian-origin import shipments in violation of 560.201 are detained, and unlicensed shipments face penalty or seizure and forfeiture. — [31 CFR 560.702 (search summary)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.702)
- **Marking fraud.** Removing or altering origin marks with intent to conceal carries up to $100,000 for a first offence and $250,000 for later offences, plus possible imprisonment. — [19 U.S.C. 1304(l)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1304)
- **OFAC v. Adani Enterprises (18 May 2026).**
  - Settlement $275,000,000; base penalty $384,208,088. The violation was 560.203(a): causing US persons to violate the ITSR.
  - A Dubai supplier "misrepresented itself as a 'middleman'" selling "Omani and Iraqi-origin" LPG that was in fact Iranian.
  - Red flags OFAC cited: discounted price, AIS manipulation, vessel name, flag or ownership changes, illogical port calls, signs of falsified shipping documents, and a bank freezing payments.
  - 32 USD payments totalling over $192 million were cleared through the US.
  - Practitioners advise verifying certificates of origin with third-party diligence.

  — [Akrivis Law Group summary](https://www.akrivislaw.com/news-insights/legal-update-ofac-settles-at-275-million-civil-penalty-with-india-based-adani-enterprises-limited-over-iranian-origin-gas-shipments)
- **OFAC v. ZAG (Feb 2019).** A UAE supplier sold Iranian-origin cement clinker to a Connecticut company, "falsely representing" it was not sanctioned. Settled for $506,250 after voluntary disclosure. — [Manufacturing Law Blog](https://www.manufacturinglawblog.com/2019/02/connecticut-company-reaches-settlement-with-ofac-regarding-alleged-iran-sanctions-violations/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=a52cc22c-3b8d-41ae-8b93-e305dd164f8b)
- **OFAC individual settlement.** It involved apparent violations of 560.206 (transactions related to Iranian-origin goods); base penalty $1,210,336; found egregious. — [Steptoe blog](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/ofac-enters-into-rare-settlement-with-individual-over-iranian-payments-and-facilitation.html)
- **Spain, saffron fraud (2021).** Police arrested 17 in a network that sold Iranian saffron as premium La Mancha product. Europol estimated about €10 million in gains. Police found over two tonnes of Iranian saffron and a clandestine packing and labelling workshop. — [Washington Post, 7 May 2021](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2021/05/07/saffron-gang-fraud/); [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/gang-made-millions-selling-fake-saffron-as-high-quality-product)
- **Spain as a saffron re-exporter.** Saffron is "repackaged, relabelled and reexported as 'packaged in Spain', 'selected and packed in Spain,' or sometimes even 'produced in Spain'." Spain is the second-largest saffron exporter largely because it imports nearly half of world production from Iran. — [Shepro, Oxford Symposium on Food & Cookery paper](https://www.oxfordsymposium.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Shepro.pdf)
- **Iranian claims on smuggling routes.**
  - Iran's saffron bodies say Iranian saffron is rerouted via Afghanistan, the UAE and Spain because direct exports to the US are unfeasible.
  - One union head claimed 90 t (40% of 225 t) was smuggled in a year.
  - These are unverified official claims.

  — [Iran International, Apr 2024](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202404215736); [Afghanistan International, Jan 2024](https://www.afintl.com/en/202401151093)
- **Iranian pistachios via Turkey (historical).** Israel indicted an importer in 1998 over Iranian nuts. Iranian pistachios routed via Turkey were reportedly relabelled Turkish or Greek. In 2010 the US asked Israel to scrutinise "Turkish" pistachios. — [Seattle Times, 1998](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19980206/2732873/irans-pistachio-sales-in-israel-drive-us-farmers-nuts); [Jerusalem Post](https://m.jpost.com/iranian-threat/news/iranian-pistachio-nuts-sold-in-israel); [WGBH/NPR 2016](https://www.wgbh.org/news/2016-04-03/pistachios-a-love-story)
- **Turkey used to transship US goods to Iran.** In the Alabama case, the defendant told suppliers that goods were for Turkish and UAE end users; he pleaded guilty. This is the mirror image (US exports to Iran), not an import case. — [DOJ, Alabama man pleads guilty](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-man-pleads-guilty-violating-iran-sanctions)

### Inferences
- **What crosses the line.** Each of these is illegal origin falsification:
  - declaring Turkey (or Afghanistan or Spain) as origin on a US or EU customs entry
  - obtaining a Turkish certificate of origin on false facts
  - using "Product of Turkey" labels on Iranian saffron or barberries
  - routing payments to hide the Iranian supplier
- **US exposure, cumulative.** On top of the ITSR penalties above, the standard US customs-fraud statutes apply: 18 U.S.C. 542 (false statements on entry), 18 U.S.C. 545 (smuggling, up to 20 years), and 19 U.S.C. 1592 (civil fraud penalty up to the domestic value of the goods). These statutes were not re-fetched this session; see Gaps.
- **EU exposure.**
  - Customs penalties are set nationally under UCC Art. 42.
  - Misleading origin information on food breaches Reg. (EU) 1169/2011 Art. 7(1)(a). Art. 26 requires origin labelling where omission would mislead.
  - Because saffron is not under EU sanctions, the EU sanctions-crime directive (2024/1226) is not triggered by the product itself. It can be triggered if payments touch EU-listed Iranian banks or persons.
- **Red flags banks and customs will look for:**
  - Turkish exporters with no saffron production base
  - "Turkish saffron" in commercial volumes (Turkey's own production is tiny; it is a net importer)
  - Iranian truck plates or TIR carnets from Iran
  - mismatched HS descriptions
  - lab or isotope testing (used in the Spanish case)
- **Exposure of the Turkish relatives.** Even non-US Turkish relatives risk 560.203 "causing" liability if any US person, the US dollar or a US platform touches the chain. The 24 August 2026 expansion of EO 13902 secondary-sanctions authority (shipping, gold and others) raises the general risk profile of Iran-linked logistics firms. That expansion does not name food.

### Gaps
- **No US case on saffron or barberries.** No DOJ or OFAC enforcement found specifically on Iranian saffron, barberries or dried fruit relabelled as Turkish, Afghan or Spanish.
- **No OLAF reports.** No OLAF report found on Iranian saffron or pistachio origin fraud.
- **Turkish penalties.** Not retrieved: penalties for false origin declarations or forged certificates of origin (Customs Law 4458, Anti-Smuggling Law 5607, Turkish Penal Code forgery articles).
- **US customs-fraud statutes.** 18 U.S.C. 542/545 and 19 U.S.C. 1592 were not re-fetched; the penalty descriptions above are standard but unverified this session.

## Q6. Lawful alternatives that still use Turkey

### Takeaway
Two structures are defensible:
- **EU sales.** Turkish relatives (non-US persons) act as a normal importer and exporter, or Turkey serves as a logistics or consolidation hub. Iranian origin is declared truthfully throughout, and EU food-safety and labelling rules are met.
- **US sales.** A non-US Turkish manufacturer makes a genuinely new product. The origin is confirmed in advance by a CBP binding ruling, and no US person is involved upstream.

Nothing lawful lets Iranian whole or ground saffron or dried barberries reach the US market.

### Cited Findings
- **EU sanctions do not bar Iranian food.** Reimposed EU Iran trade restrictions (Sept 2025) target dual-use, energy, naval, software, graphite and metals goods, not food. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-reintroduces-sanctions-against-iran-following-un-snapback); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/corporate-governance-compliance/art/eu-iran-reimposing-sanctions)
- **The US safe harbour exists only for products manufactured or substantially transformed by non-US persons.** — [31 CFR 560.407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.407)
- **US persons cannot facilitate.** Section 560.208 bars US persons from facilitating foreign persons' Iran-goods transactions. — [31 CFR 560.208](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/560.208)
- **Trade channels exist.** Turkey legally imports Iranian saffron, and the Iran–Turkey PTA gives tariff concessions on Iranian farm goods. — [BAKA 2024](https://baka.gov.tr/assets/upload/dosyalar/safran.pdf); [ADASO](https://adaso.org.tr/Content/WebDosyalar/Yayinlar/DisTicaretRaporlari/T%C3%9CRK%C4%B0YE%20%20%C4%B0RAN%20TERC%C4%B0HL%C4%B0%20T%C4%B0CARET%20ANLA%C5%9EMASI-ANAL%C4%B0Z-.pdf)

### Inferences
- **EU route A (transit only).** Iran to Turkey under TIR/transit, onward to an EU importer of record. The EU entry declares "IR" as origin. There is no Turkish duty and no change of origin.
- **EU route B (Turkish import and re-export).** A Turkish relatives' company imports into Turkey under the PTA, sorts and packs, and exports to the EU. The Turkish certificate of origin and the EU declaration both say Iran. The pack says "Origin: Iran; packed in Türkiye."
  - Label as "packed in Türkiye" only, never "Product of Türkiye," per 1169/2011 Art. 26 logic.
  - Banking: avoid USD, and screen Iranian counterparties and banks against EU, UN and US SDN lists.
- **EU route C (Turkish-origin product).** Turkish-origin confectionery or tea blends with saffron, labelled honestly. If labels emphasise "Iranian saffron," the primary-ingredient origin rules (Reg. 2018/775) apply.
- **US route (manufactured product).**
  - Only a genuinely manufactured product made by a non-US Turkish producer.
  - Obtain a CBP binding ruling (19 CFR Part 177) on substantial transformation before shipping.
  - Consider asking OFAC for interpretive guidance under 560.407.
  - If the couple are US persons, they may only buy the finished product at arm's length. They must not source, finance or direct the Iranian-input purchasing or processing.
- **Payment and finance risk.** Turkish banks often de-risk Iranian flows regardless of legality, as the Halkbank history shows (not researched in this session).

### Gaps
- **Not verified:** the exact Turkish customs steps (transit declaration types, import control certificate, PTA proof of origin form) and Turkish VAT and duty costs for Route B.
- **Not verified:** whether Turkish banks will currently process Iran-linked food payments in practice, and current enforcement against Turkish banks (Halkbank case status in 2026 not researched).
